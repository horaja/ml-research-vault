**core**

- [Beyond Attention or Similarity: Maximizing Conditional Diversity for Token Pruning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b23dd0634e7de523598df0a907a5bf5) — Zhang, Liu, Li et al. · NeurIPS · 103 cites
  Attention-based pruning retains many similar high-attention tokens; similarity-based pruning maximizes diversity but ignores instruction semantics. This paper maximizes *conditional* diversity given the instruction text, forcing the selected set to be both relevant and non-redundant. NeurIPS, 103 cites: this is the baseline SURF must beat on instruction-conditioned selection. Critical question: is the "conditioning" in conditional diversity compositional (token relevance changes with instruction meaning), or retrieval-like (instruction acts as a key)? If retrieval-like, this baseline shares the failure mode Chen et al. (2026-10-01) identified in VLAs.

- [TReVS: Integrating Textual Relevance and Visual Saliency for Efficient Vision-Language Models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1e84ab85eee5e2be54de00c3fe8d3fb) — Wang, Wu, Ren et al. · preprint
  Two-stage: visual saliency first (removes visually redundant tokens post-encoder), then text-relevance pruning (removes tokens irrelevant to the instruction pre-LLM). Directly publishes SURF's two-stage architecture without geometric structure. The gap SURF claims is structural geometry vs. generic saliency as the visual prior. Check: does TReVS show the two-stage approach outperforms single-stage text-only or saliency-only baselines? If yes, SURF's architecture is validated; the remaining question is which visual prior wins.

- [Select, Compress, Reinvest: A Controlled Study of Visual-Token Allocation in Long-Video LLMs](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/995038e2c94438f095727fdbc18239a0) — Khatri · preprint
  Published selectors are hard to compare because they simultaneously change the selection criterion AND the compression ratio; this paper isolates one variable. The result is a methodological argument that SURF's ablation must hold compression ratio fixed and vary only the structural prior vs. baselines. Required reading before writing [[02-projects/surf/experiments/contrastive alignment]] spec — the confound it identifies is the primary threat to any published SURF comparison.

**adjacent**

- [Theory of Space: Can Foundation Models Construct Spatial Beliefs through Active Perception?](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/349a6bbf00dcf13be0904a8253afd13d) — Zhang, Huang, Wang et al. (Fei-Fei Li, Ranjay Krishna, Jia-Jun Wu) · arXiv · 23 cites
  Tests whether foundation models can actively acquire spatial information via self-directed exploration under partial observability; introduces oracle gap as a metric. Frames SURF's core premise as a testable evaluation: can SURF's adaptive selection close the oracle gap on spatial tasks that require choosing which regions to observe? The oracle gap metric is a candidate evaluation criterion for [[02-projects/surf/claims/iterative selection]] — does iterative selection converge toward oracle token maps?

- [End-to-end topographic networks as models of cortical map formation and human vision](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b8bf7404003befc7d91df4fd193b812) — Lu, Doerig, Bosch, Krahmer, Kaiser, Cichy, Kietzmann · Nature Human Behaviour · 28 cites
  Shows that locality + feature-similarity constraints applied end-to-end reproduce primate visual cortex topographic organization, including V4-like shape/texture maps; ImageNet-trained CNNs without topographic constraints do not. Directly relevant to [[02-projects/surf/claims/v4 relevance]] and Dr. Lee's action item on V4 topo. If V4's topographic organization emerges from spatial locality + feature similarity, SURF's structural prior (which enforces spatial coherence in selection) has a principled biological analog beyond just "line drawings look like edges."

**horizon**

- [JEPA Learns What the Mask Leaves Unrecoverable](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61eec504ce5432e35b648e6f26588cd1d7b5eb70) — Xie, Alanwar · preprint
  Measurement-theoretic account of why block masks work in JEPA: in a compactly supported wavelet basis, every atom inside a block-masked region falls in the measurement's null space — completely unrecoverable, not interpolable. Scattered masks leave information recoverable via spatial interpolation. This is the first theoretical account of why spatially coherent selection is informationally distinct from random selection — the blocked region is a hard information wall, not noise. Supports [[02-projects/surf/claims/structure helps]] at the measurement-theory level.

- [A single computational objective can produce specialization of streams in visual cortex](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f673f711af4d48c03135e75c4cf08a74fa30e653) — Finzi, Margalit, Kay, Yamins, Grill-Spector · bioRxiv · 7 cites
  A self-supervised Topographic DAN trained on a single objective reproduces dorsal, lateral, and ventral stream specialization in a massive fMRI dataset — stream differentiation is emergent from topographic constraints alone, not from separate task objectives. Relevant to [[02-projects/surf/claims/v4 relevance]]: if functional specialization in visual cortex emerges from topographic locality constraints, then SURF's structural prior (spatially local selection) approximates the same computational constraint that generates V4's selectivity. Weakens the argument that V4-like selectivity requires explicit shape/geometry supervision.

## questions

New from today:
- Beyond Attention: Is "conditional diversity" conditioned compositionally on instruction meaning, or does it use the instruction as a retrieval key? If retrieval-style, does this baseline replicate the failure mode Chen et al. identified?
- TReVS: What is the "visual saliency" signal — softmax attention, edge density, or learned? Does the two-stage approach outperform single-stage text-only and vision-only baselines on the same evaluation protocol?
- Select/Compress/Reinvest: Does SURF's current [[02-projects/surf/experiments/contrastive alignment]] spec already isolate selection criterion from compression ratio, or does it conflate them?
- End-to-end topographic nets: Does the emergent V4-like topography in the model match the shape/texture balance from Jiang et al. (dispersity measure), or is it broader spatial organization?
- Visual streams from single objective: Does the TDANN reproduce V4-specific shape/texture selectivity, or just the coarser dorsal/ventral functional split?

Carried from 2026-10-01:
- CoVisco: Is codec-native token compression structurally biased (spatial layout) or semantic/temporal?
- When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories: What distinguishes retrieval-style from compositional language conditioning, and does SURF's contrastive scoring avoid retrieval-style by design?
- Self-distillation for compressed context: Is it agnostic to the compression criterion (compatible with SURF's structural selector), or does it require knowing which tokens were removed?
- EWAM: Does SURF's input-stage token reduction interact with EWAM-style depth-wise specialization, or are horizontal token width and vertical depth orthogonal?
- Tactile curiosity oracle: On manipulation benchmarks, do SURF's structural line-drawing selections preferentially score contact-adjacent patches?
- TLC-DiT: What defines "task-aligned"?
- NeuronEye: Does V4-inspired disentanglement improve task-grounded token selection vs. just visual Q&A?
- Instruction-conditioned sparsification (Cao et al.): sparsification criterion — semantic similarity, spatial attention, or learned? (day 3)
- GSPA: geo component still unknown (day 109)
