S2 is back today (s2_keyword: 410, s2_recommend: 65 — first full-corpus digest since Nov 1; prior 3 days were arXiv + RSS only). The full pipeline surfaced three papers that directly engage SURF's architectural premises from distinct angles.

A³-VLA (arXiv 2611.06447) confirms the core problem: VLA attention drifts to task-irrelevant shortcuts because action prediction lacks fine-grained perceptual supervision. Their solution (automated segmentation-guided attention supervision during fine-tuning) is a training-time fix; SURF's structural prior is an unsupervised inference-time fix. The comparison is direct: does a structural prior at the patch level achieve comparable attention alignment without task-specific supervision labels?

NEO-VLA (arXiv 2611.06918) removes the ViT encoder entirely and processes raw image patches inline with the LLM in a Mamba-Transformer hybrid. This reframes the stage-placement question: if the encoder is removed, structural priors must be applied at the patch tokenization stage inside the LLM, not at a separate ViT pre-encoder. The paper explicitly motivates this by noting that standard VLA pipelines lose local geometry, boundaries, and depth cues in the visual encoder compression — exactly the cues SURF's structural prior is designed to preserve. Encoder-free design is a potential SURF variant worth noting in the positioning note.

Where Is VLA Inference Time Spent? (arXiv 2611.06710) profiles VLA inference bottlenecks across hardware. SURF's pre-encoder token selection reduces ViT encoding cost and, more importantly, reduces LLM prefill length by reducing the number of visual tokens fed to the LLM. If LLM prefill is the dominant bottleneck (expected for large-backbone VLAs), token count reduction at the visual encoder output is the most impactful efficiency intervention — this paper provides the empirical grounding to make that claim.

**core**

- [A³-VLA: Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06447) — Zhang et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  VLA attention drifts to task-irrelevant shortcuts because action prediction lacks fine-grained perceptual supervision. A³-VLA uses a referring segmentation foundation model to auto-generate task-specific attention supervision during fine-tuning. Direct comparison point for SURF: a training-time supervised fix vs. SURF's inference-time unsupervised structural prior. The key diagnostic question: does a structural prior at the patch level achieve comparable attention alignment without task-specific labels? Read to extract which manipulation tasks show the worst attention drift and how their supervised map compares to what a line-drawing prior would produce.

- [Where Is VLA Inference Time Spent? Bottlenecks and Optimization Interactions Across GPUs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06710) — Waseem et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Profiles VLA inference across GPU hardware, measuring interactions between quantization, pruning, compilation (CUDA graphs, kernel fusion). Key for SURF: SURF's pre-encoder token selection reduces ViT encoding cost and — crucially — reduces LLM prefill length by passing fewer visual tokens. If LLM prefill dominates (likely for large-backbone VLAs like OpenVLA), reducing visual token count is the highest-leverage efficiency intervention. Read to extract the ViT vs. LLM prefill vs. action decode breakdown by VLA architecture — directly determines what SURF's efficiency claim is competing against.

- [From Pixels to Actions — Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06918) — Fan et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  NEO-VLA: removes the ViT encoder, tokenizes images as raw patches via lightweight convolutions inside a Mamba-Transformer hybrid, with visual and action reasoning sharing the same computational graph. Explicitly motivated by the loss of local geometry, boundaries, and depth cues under standard ViT compression. This reframes SURF's stage-placement question: in an encoder-free design, structural priors apply at the patch tokenization stage, not at a separate pre-encoder. The design also makes structural patch selection end-to-end differentiable — a possible SURF variant. Read to understand the patch tokenization design and what performance gap (if any) remains vs. ViT-based VLAs.

**adjacent**

- [Visual Token Routing: Learning to keep tokens that matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06854) — Aggarwal et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Trains a token router for computer-use agents (CUAs) to drop semantically irrelevant and temporally redundant visual tokens across long interaction histories; router is learned end-to-end from downstream task signal. Different domain from VLA manipulation, but the routing design space is SURF-adjacent: downstream-task routing (this paper) vs. structural prior routing (SURF's approach). The "temporally redundant" axis (tokens unchanged from prior step) is orthogonal to SURF's spatial structural axis — combining temporal and structural pruning is a possible extension. The end-to-end learning (vs. SURF's contrastive prior) also frames a direct architectural contrast with [[02-projects/surf/claims/language helps]].

- [Exploiting Biases in Attention for Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04657) — Herr et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Tokens in ViTs have absolute importance for attention independently of queries; this phenomenon grows with depth. Reformulates attention with an input-dependent bias term that makes intrinsic importance explicit and learnable. Adjacent to SURF's premise: if tokens have inherent, query-independent importance, structural patches (edges, object boundaries) may be persistently important regardless of task instruction — the structural prior is then extracting a real signal, not just a heuristic. The depth-dependence finding is relevant to stage placement: intrinsic importance becomes increasingly reliable deeper in the network, which may favor post-language-fusion selection over SURF's pre-encoder placement.

**horizon**

- [Interactive World Simulation with Cosmos for Physical AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06627) — NVIDIA Research · arXiv Nov 2026
  Adapts Cosmos world foundation models for real-time interactive world simulation in Physical AI: streams visual observations in response to robot actions, enabling online synthetic data generation and policy training without physical robot access. Horizon relevance: Cosmos-class world simulation changes the data assumptions for training any VLA or selector module — SURF's contrastive prior could be trained on Cosmos-generated rollouts, dramatically expanding the available training distribution. Also relevant to eval: Cosmos simulation provides controllable scenes where structural-prior ablations (line-drawing vs. depth vs. random) can be evaluated under distribution shift without sim-to-real cost.
