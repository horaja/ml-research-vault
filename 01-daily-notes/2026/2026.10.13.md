#### Core

**Poster: Task-Aware Dynamic Visual Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Inference** — Lin, Sun, Pang (s2_keyword)
TADP is training-free: estimates task-conditioned token importance after early vision-language interaction, decouples main/wrist-view budgets, and reuses keyframe scores under temporal consistency. As a training-free method operating post-vision-language fusion, TADP occupies exactly the regime SURF targets; the question is whether geometric line priors add anything that text-conditioned attention scoring misses when the model has not seen the manipulation object before.

**Focus-Scan-Refine: From Human Visual Perception to Efficient Visual Token Pruning** — Tong, Bai, Zhu, Jiang, Liu (s2_keyword)
Mimics human foveal perception with a three-stage plug-and-play framework: focus on key evidence, scan globally, refine under local/global conflict. The closest architectural analogue to SURF's coarse-to-fine structural prior among current VLM pruning approaches — it imports the same biological foveal principle but implements it via attention dynamics rather than geometric line priors. Whether FSR's attention-based "focus" stage targets the same spatial regions as SURF's structural selection on manipulation scenes is the patch-overlap check that determines whether the bio-grounding argument is independent or redundant.

**Beyond Attention or Similarity: Maximizing Conditional Diversity for Token Pruning in MLLMs** — Zhang, Liu, Li, Lu et al. (s2_keyword)
Argues that attention-based pruning retains duplicate tokens and similarity-based pruning ignores instruction relevance; proposes maximizing conditional diversity — retaining tokens diverse conditioned on the instruction — as the selection objective. Directly probes [[02-projects/surf/claims/language helps]]: if instruction-conditioned diversity is a sufficient selection criterion, a geometric prior must demonstrate contribution beyond what text-conditioned diversity maximization already recovers.

#### Adjacent

**Deep learning-driven characterization of single cell tuning in primate visual area V4 supports topological organization** — Willeke, Restivo, Cadena, Tolias et al. (s2_keyword)
Uses predictive encoding models to characterize V4 single-cell tuning for natural images and finds column-like topographic organization — extending the V4 topographic map from parametric stimuli to natural scene space. Directly intersects @jiangComputationalConstraintsUnderlying: if topographic columns hold under natural images, a per-patch shape score derived from V4-like dispersity is geometrically consistent rather than stimulus-specific. Whether Willeke et al.'s per-column features are compatible with the digital twin pipeline from @wangLargescaleCalciumImaging2024 is the tractability question for the dispersity-as-prior approach.

**End-to-end topographic networks as models of cortical map formation and human visual behaviour** — Lu, Doerig, Bosch, Krahmer, Kaiser, Cichy, Kietzmann (s2_keyword)
Introduces All-TNNs trained end-to-end that develop topographic visual features matching the primate cortex, showing topographic organization emerges from the visual task objective rather than hand-designed locality constraints. For [[02-projects/surf/claims/v4 relevance]]: if topographic priors are learnable end-to-end, a V4-aligned selection module need not hardcode the neuroscience map — it can be trained to recover it, which lowers the engineering bar for SURF's prior module and strengthens the grounding argument.

**Visual Anagrams Reveal Hidden Differences in Holistic Shape Processing Across Vision Models** — Doshi, Fel, Konkle, Alvarez (s2_keyword)
Uses visual anagrams (images whose interpretation flips under gestalt reorganization) to show that current vision models fail at holistic object-part configuration even when controlling for texture — the deficit is not the shape/texture tradeoff but an absolute gap in compositional shape encoding. Supports [[02-projects/surf/claims/structure helps]] from the recognition axis: if models systematically miss configurational shape geometry, a selection prior that biases toward shape-informative patches (line drawings) addresses a genuine structural deficit in the backbone rather than adding redundant signal.

#### Horizon

**When Do Early-Exit Networks Generalize? A PAC-Bayesian Theory of Adaptive Depth** — Guo, Wu, Yiu (s2_keyword)
Proves generalization bounds for early-exit networks depending on exit-depth entropy H(D): adaptive computation generalizes when exit decisions are maximally uncertain across the dataset, and degrades when all samples are forced to early or late exits. The entropy-based bound provides a theoretical criterion for SURF's iterative selection stopping policy — the loop should stop when the remaining patch representation no longer reduces the posterior uncertainty over task-relevant regions, not at a fixed compression ratio, which maps onto the H(D) condition.

**Self-Certification of Representation Adequacy: Sequential Certification at Minimum Task Loss** — Huang (s2_keyword)
Develops a formal theory of when a compressed representation is adequate for action: adequate iff it does not alias histories with different Bayes-optimal actions, certifiable sequentially from the agent's own transcript. Connects SURF's iterative visual selection to a decision-theoretic stopping condition — the selection loop is adequate (should stop) when the patch set no longer aliases task-discriminative visual features, formalizing "iterative selection" as sequential certification against a task loss lower bound.
