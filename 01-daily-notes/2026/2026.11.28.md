**core**

- [ProbFaS: Probabilistic Video Frame Selection for MLLMs via Multi-Fidelity Calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34903) — Zhang et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  ITA-score-based frame selectors are systematically overconfident at certain appearance states; ProbFaS applies multi-fidelity calibration to assign high-confidence frames lower-resolution processing and uncertain frames full-resolution. Direct warning for SURF's contrastive selector: the patch-language compatibility scorer may be similarly overconfident on textureless or center-heavy patches, selecting with false certainty. The multi-fidelity mechanism (adaptive fidelity per-patch based on calibrated score confidence) is transferable from temporal to spatial selection.

**adjacent**

- [Memory Is Not Ground Truth: Revising Navigation Authority under Unreliable VLM Evidence for Persistent Semantic Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34915) — arXiv (Nov 2026)
  When a VLM-guided navigation memory consistently misses a target object, distinguishing "object absent" from "VLM failing on this appearance" is ambiguous under repeated sensing. They show the ambiguity is systematic (VLM failure correlates with specific scene configurations). Directly frames [[02-projects/surf/claims/iterative selection]]'s when-to-stop criterion: not all selector misses justify requerying — the selector should know its failure-mode distribution to decide whether to refine or stop.

- [Tracking Any Point with Agentic Visual Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34943) — arXiv (Nov 2026)
  An agentic VLM (search + verify loop) surpasses specialized point-tracking models at pixel-level correspondence, without task-specific training. Soft threat to SURF's design assumption: if the VLA backbone's native spatial reasoning is powerful enough to track individual points agentically, explicit pre-encoder structural selection may add less value than expected — the VLA may implicitly select the right visual evidence given the right prompting.

- [RePace: Reasoning about Demonstration Speedup for Faster Vision-Language-Action Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34864) — Lee & Jeon · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  LLM reasons about which VLA training demo segments need less execution precision; selectively speeds them up before fine-tuning, yielding faster policies without a deployment-time module. Training-time analog of SURF's inference-time claim: LLM-based importance scoring over temporal segments validates that precision requirements vary across a task execution — applies to spatial patches at inference time by analogy.
