**core**

- [CoVisco: Codec-Native Vision Encoder with Native Token Compression for Unified Image-Video Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39924v1) — Liu, Han, Shang · arXiv · 2026-09-30
  Codec-native token compression during encoding rather than post-hoc compression of dense encoder output; removes the train/inference mismatch that standard compression introduces. Directly maps to SURF's architectural slot: if native compression inside the encoder achieves structural-token selection without an explicit prior module, SURF's modular approach needs to argue what the structural prior adds over codec-level compression. Read to extract: is the native compression structurally biased (preserving spatial layout), or is it semantic? Does it generalize to manipulation-relevant geometric content?

- [When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories: Diagnosing and Mitigating Generalization Failures in VLA Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39971v1) — Chen, Hou, Chen · arXiv · 2026-09-30
  VLA models fail when language instructions are correlated with specific trajectories in training data and act as retrieval keys rather than compositional visual selection signals; proposes diagnostic and mitigation. Threatens [[claims/language helps]]: if instruction-conditioned token selection acts as retrieval rather than composition, SURF's contrastive scoring stage replicates the same failure. Read to extract: what distinguishes retrieval-style from compositional conditioning, and does the mitigation require changing the contrastive objective or the training distribution?

- [Learning to Reason with Compressed Context: Ground-Truth-Free Adaptation of OmniLLMs via Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39953v1) — Wang, Meng, Li · arXiv · 2026-09-30
  Adapts the language model to reason effectively from compressed multimodal token sequences via self-distillation, without requiring ground-truth labels for which tokens to keep. Completes the SURF pipeline argument: if the downstream LLM co-adapts to compressed visual input, SURF's structural prior selection is more justified and the performance floor from compression rises. Check: is the self-distillation agnostic to the compression criterion (compatible with SURF's structural selector), or does it require knowing which tokens were removed at training time?

**adjacent**

- [EWAM: Emergent Depth-Wise Specialization in a Unified Embodied Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39973v1) — Wang, Wen, Liu · arXiv · 2026-09-30
  Unified VLA+WAM where asymmetric joint attention causes semantic understanding, visual foresight, and action generation to emerge at different depth ranges — depth-wise vertical specialization. SURF's horizontal token width reduction is the input analog: SURF selects which tokens enter, EWAM's depth-wise specialization selects which layers each token traverses. Dual-axis adaptive compute picture: fewer tokens in × fewer layers processed = the full efficiency argument. Check: does EWAM's depth specialization interact with token count at the input stage, or are these orthogonal?

- [Tactile Curiosity Drives Robot Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40134v1) — Iten, Proshkin, Sukhija · arXiv · 2026-09-30
  Allocates RL exploration budget toward contact-rich spatial regions rather than free-space motions via tactile curiosity signal; improves sample efficiency for manipulation tasks. Grounds SURF's contact-affordance argument: contact regions are the information-dense spatial zones in manipulation, and SURF's structural prior should preferentially select patches there. Tactile curiosity provides an RL-derived oracle for which spatial regions carry task-relevant information — candidate ground truth for evaluating structural prior quality against contact proximity on manipulation benchmarks.

**horizon**

- [Scaling Laws for Looped Mixture of Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40316v1) — Chen, Goyal, Krishnamoorthi · arXiv · 2026-09-30
  First joint scaling law modeling both recurrence depth and MoE sparsity; finds they interact: optimal depth-to-sparsity ratio shifts with total compute budget. SURF's [[claims/iterative selection]] is a form of recurrent visual processing; the visual budget is the effective token count per step. A scaling law for recurrence × sparsity trade-off is directly relevant to sizing SURF's iterate-vs-stop criterion: at what compute budget does iterative refinement outperform a single-pass deeper encoder?
