**The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use** (Wang et al., arXiv 2608.06270) is today's most important calibration paper for surf. The "thinking-with-images" paradigm — equipping MLLMs with active crop-and-zoom operations — produces marginal or negative gains over direct inference at substantially higher token cost. The paper performs a causal audit: replacing the visual evidence returned by the crop-and-zoom with a blank or irrelevant patch does not systematically degrade the answer, meaning the model is not actually using the returned evidence causally. This is a null result against the entire paradigm surf builds on. The mechanism-level distinction surf must make explicit: surf's structural prior operates at the *pre-inference token selection* stage, not as an in-context visual tool; the contrastive scorer is trained to select tokens that the transformer *will* attend to, not to return a zoomed-in view it may or may not use. This paper surfaces the exact failure mode surf's architecture must avoid — the distinction between "token selected before computation" and "evidence retrieved during generation" is the key architectural claim the spec's introduction needs to make explicit.

Compressor-VLA (Gao et al., S2 84c824...) has now been unread for 31 days. It tests instruction-guided VT compression for VLA *without* a structural geometry prior — the exact configuration that isolates [[claims/language helps]] from [[claims/structure helps]]. Writing the spec differentiation section without reading this paper is not defensible.

## questions

- Illusion of Visual Tool-Use × [[claims/iterative selection]] causal mechanism: Wang et al. show crop-and-zoom evidence is not causally used. Does surf's pre-inference token selection escape this null result? The mechanism argument is: structural tokens are selected *before* the transformer encodes the visual sequence, so they are causally present in the input rather than appended as tool-call evidence mid-generation. Is this distinction testable — does a version where surf's selected tokens are appended as a crop-and-zoom output rather than replacing the input token sequence fail on the same causal audit?
- Compressor-VLA × [[claims/language helps]] vs [[claims/structure helps]] isolation: If Compressor-VLA achieves competitive task success on LIBERO via language-conditioned compression alone, what specific failure modes does it exhibit that surf's structural geometry prior corrects? Are those failures measurable on publicly available LIBERO data before writing the spec?
- GSPA × spec novelty gap: GSPA combines geometric + semantic priors for long-horizon VLA. Does it use a fixed, training-free geometric prior or a learned geometric prior? Is the semantic prior instruction-conditioned or task-agnostic? The novelty gap for surf depends on the precise answer — surf's claim is that a contrastive alignment between structural geometry and language instruction is missing from GSPA.
- Depth Matters × [[claims/structure helps]] prior comparison: Zhu et al. use depth supervision to train a decoupled VLA encoder. Is depth supervision available at VLA inference time on LIBERO, or is it training-only? If training-only, the runtime prior comparison is: depth-trained encoder vs. line-density inference-time prior — different settings, not a clean ablation. What is the correct comparison protocol?
- GeniWorld × contrastive scorer target: GeniWorld's visual-action prediction target (flow, motion, keypoints) captures action-relevant patches better than RGB. Should surf's contrastive scorer training signal include a motion-feature component alongside structural geometry — not as a competing prior but as a complementary supervision signal for the same patches?
