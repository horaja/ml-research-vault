S2 rate limiting continued through today (day 5 consecutive). Bypassed with arXiv + RSS only; digest is valid but misses S2 keyword, recommendations, and author feeds.

Three papers of immediate design relevance today. NoveltyGate (2611.31099) is the most important: it shows that the standard assumption — route tokens with the highest approximation error to full computation — is wrong for VLA prefix selection. High-error tokens concentrate in views the policy relies on little (third-person camera); high-novelty tokens concentrate in views the policy depends on heavily (wrist camera). This is an error-reliance gap, and it directly constrains SURF's selector criterion. If SURF's contrastive prior scores tokens by reconstruction deviation or attention magnitude, it may be selecting for the wrong signal. The novelty test (whether a token's input falls outside the calibration subspace) is the operationally correct signal — read this paper before finalizing the SURF selector design.

HIVE-Bench (2611.30813) adds evaluation methodology: a single policy reading dense patch tokens from 20 encoders across 24 egocentric bimanual tasks. Key finding: averaging each view to one token removes most task success. The inverse-dynamics readout of commanded motion is one of the strongest predictors of downstream performance before training. This is a reusable evaluation frame for SURF's selection quality.

Sink Token (2611.31025) adds a constraint on SURF's pruning design: prefix-token-free backbones (like SigLIP 2) form sink tokens on patch tokens, and removing them collapses predictions and coherence. SURF's pre-encoder selection must preserve whatever serves as the sink, even if it's a patch rather than a prefix.

**core**

- [NoveltyGate: Novelty, Not Error, Decides Which VLA Prefix Tokens to Compute Exactly](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31099) — Qi · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Demonstrates an error-reliance gap in π_{0.5}: high-approximation-error tokens (third-person camera) are the ones the policy relies on least; high-novelty tokens (wrist camera, things outside calibration subspace) are the ones that matter. Training-free; routes each prefix token to low-rank or exact MLP based on novelty before any multiplication. 1.61× speedup on π_{0.5} prefix with closed-loop success preserved. **Critical design constraint for SURF's selector criterion**: if selection is based on reconstruction error or attention magnitude rather than input novelty, it is likely selecting the wrong tokens. Read before finalizing [[02-projects/surf/experiments/contrastive alignment]].

- [HIVE-Bench: Evaluating Patch-Level Visual Representations for Egocentric Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30813) — Liang et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Benchmarks 20 vision encoders across 24 bimanual egocentric manipulation tasks using a shared flow-matching policy that reads dense patch tokens. Averaging views to one vector removes most task success. The inverse-dynamics readout (commanded motion prediction from patch features before policy training) is one of the strongest predictors of downstream performance. Direct evaluation methodology for SURF: does SURF's V4 prior select the patches whose inverse-dynamics readout is highest? This is a tractable, pre-training measure of selection quality.

- [Token Pruning Needs a Protected Sink Token: Coherent Spatial Aggregation in Vision Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31025) — Wyatt et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Finds that coherent token pruning requires protecting "sink" tokens — high-norm tokens that absorb and redistribute non-local spatial information. Prefix-token models (DINOv3) are coherent by construction; prefix-free models (SigLIP 2) form sinks on patch tokens, and removing them collapses coherence. MCTP (Monte Carlo Token Pruning) measures this empirically. SURF constraint: if the target backbone is SigLIP 2 or similar, pre-encoder selection must identify and preserve patch-level sinks, not just the highest-scoring patches.

**adjacent**

- [WeaveVLA: 3D Relational Grounding for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31126) — Wang et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Adds a task-conditioned sparse GNN encoding relative 3D relations among objects, gripper, and reference points; relational tokens condition the action expert alongside vision-language context. Gains 7.1 pp on LIBERO-Plus over π_{0.5}, with larger gains under OOD perturbations. Direct competitor to SURF's spatial structure approach: WeaveVLA's relational tokens require 3D reconstruction while SURF's V4 prior is pre-encoder and training-distribution-free. OOD gains need to be compared head-to-head.

- [Modern Self-Supervised Vision Transformers Are Pareidolic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31238) — Lin et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Patch-level self-supervised losses (DINOv2/v3) produce a "pareidolic subspace" that dominates feature variance on white noise and suppresses object emergence on natural images. PSC removes this bias training-free, recovering up to 31.7 CorLoc. SURF implication: if the target backbone has a large pareidolic subspace, its patch importance estimates are biased toward the pareidolic directions rather than object/affordance structure — a V4 structural prior, which does not share this bias, may compensate precisely for this.

- [Pruning Autonomous Driving VLAs: What to Preserve for Reasoning and Action](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31240) — Kim et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Structured pruning of VLAs for autonomous driving using MoOP: combines reasoning (LingoQA) and trajectory (NAVSIM) importance ranks for the VLM backbone, and aggregates stepwise MLP channel importance for the action expert. At 24% parameter reduction, MoOP preserves both objectives; at 30% and above, reasoning collapses for all other methods. SURF relevance: dual-objective importance scoring as an analog to SURF's bifurcated selection criterion (instruction relevance vs. action relevance). The stepwise aggregation pattern is worth reading alongside NoveltyGate.

**horizon**

- [Seeking in Space: Benchmarking Purposeful Active Exploration of Vision-Language Models for Spatial Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31215) — Liu et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  ActSpace-Bench: 994 questions across 234 exploration episodes in 72 real-scanned indoor scenes. Strongest VLMs seek evidence with purpose but lose it to limited memory; weaker models explore no better than a random walk. Protocol separates evidence-seeking from spatial reasoning. Relevant to SURF's [[02-projects/surf/questions]] on active perception framing (Tianqin's question-conditioned attention problem): same design tension between purposeful selection and memory persistence, now with a benchmark.
