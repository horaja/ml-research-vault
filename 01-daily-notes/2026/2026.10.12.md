## opener

Positioning note is day 48. No new structural blockers reported since 10/11.

The competitive landscape has crystallized around three data-driven "where to look" alternatives: (1) stage-aware selection (When and What to Prune — different token sets at different inference stages), (2) proprioception-gated selection (ThinkProprio — robot state discretized into VLM tokens to gate visual patches), and (3) action-emergent ROI (Attention from Action — label-free ROI from gripper/motion events). The positioning note must now answer a sharp question: **when does a geometric zero-shot prior beat each of these?** Provisional answers: (1) for novel objects or tasks without stage labels, (2) when proprioceptive coverage of task-relevant patches is incomplete, (3) in low-data regimes or on objects absent from training demonstrations.

A secondary structural concern: VLA-ACL and COSTGRAD both score tokens by their direct contribution to the action head. If action-consistent tokens and line-drawing-selected patches are disjoint on manipulation scenes, SURF's structural prior selects wrong tokens for the control objective regardless of geometric soundness. Required check remains unverified.

#### Core

**Beyond Token Importance: Preserving Spatial Scaffolds for Efficient VLA Inference** — Chen, Gao et al. (arXiv 2609.36967, s2_recommend)
Argues that VLA pruning selecting tokens by semantic relevance overlooks the spatial information required for manipulation. Constructs a "Stride" baseline — uniform token sampling along the flattened 1D sequence — and shows it outperforms semantic and random pruning at moderate compression ratios, but collapses at high ratios where structural scaffolding disappears. Independent empirical support for [[02-projects/surf/claims/structure helps]]: spatial layout carries information semantic scoring discards. Open question: does the Stride collapse boundary correspond to the regime where SURF's geometric prior becomes necessary rather than merely helpful?

**Look, Focus, Act: Efficient and Robust Robot Learning via Human Gaze and Foveated Vision Transformers** — Chuang, Lee, Gao et al. (arXiv 2507.15833, s2_keyword)
GIAVA system: equips an ALOHA robot with a head-mounted gaze tracker and a foveated ViT that increases resolution at the gaze-indicated region. Claims improved efficiency and robustness over uniform-resolution baselines. The empirical axis (gaze → fovea → robustness) is the closest prior result to SURF's structural prior argument in a real robot learning setting. Should enter the positioning note baseline table alongside ThinkProprio under "data-driven spatial selection with hardware prior." Open question: is the foveated region defined by a geometric model of the gaze fixation (edge-enhanced, resolution pyramid) or by a pure crop — that distinction determines overlap with SURF's line-drawing prior.

**TLC-DiT: Task-Aligned Local Visual Conditioning for Robust Multitask Robot Manipulation** — Cai, Ichiwara, Wang, Lu et al. (arXiv 2609.34297, s2_keyword)
Plug-in extension of a DiT-based VLA policy that adds an explicit task-guided local visual feature map extracted before the backbone, without altering the backbone architecture. Motivates the module by arguing that task-relevant local visual evidence hidden inside backbone layers makes the policy fragile and uninterpretable. Closest architectural parallel to SURF's prior module: both extract explicit local structure and inject it as a conditioning signal. Key difference: TLC-DiT uses task-label conditioning to locate relevant local regions; SURF uses a geometric (line-drawing) prior that does not require task labels. This makes TLC-DiT a direct competitor for the language-conditioned prior module. Check: does TLC-DiT use spatial structure extraction (edges, contours) or purely attention-based localization?

#### Adjacent

**TARL: Learning Task-Adaptive Visual-Language Relevancy for Language-Conditioned Manipulation** — Luo, Wu, Liu et al. (s2_keyword, no arXiv ID available)
Proposes a learnable relevancy refinement module that adapts frozen VLM visual-language attention for language-conditioned manipulation using RGB-D input, with paraphrase consistency regularization for linguistic robustness. If TARL achieves strong manipulation results by adapting frozen VLM attention without a structural prior, it sharpens the question for [[02-projects/surf/claims/language helps]]: does SURF's geometric prior add measurable benefit over language-adapted attention alone? Add to reading list before writing [[02-projects/surf/claims/language helps]].

**LapMerge: Differentiable Exact-Budget Token Merging in Linear Time** — Trędowicz, Nowak, Steifer, Struski (arXiv 2610.13858, arXiv 2026)
Linear-time differentiable merging operator that uses a learned mass distribution and Hungarian (linear assignment) matching to merge token sequences with an exact budget constraint. Resolves the pairwise vs. all-at-once information trade-off in prior token merging work. Relevant to [[02-projects/surf/experiments/contrastive alignment]] design: if SURF's contrastive selection objective requires differentiable budget enforcement, LapMerge's linear-assignment operator is a drop-in candidate. Check compatibility with geometric prior masks.

**GuidedAttention: Interpretable and Correctable Visual Attention for OOD-Robust Robot Manipulation** — Murooka, Nakajo, Shirai, Motoda (arXiv 2607.21049, s2_keyword)
Introduces interpretable visual attention keypoints as an explicit intermediate representation in visuomotor IL; a diffusion policy is conditioned on predicted keypoints that users can inspect and correct at rollout initialization. Reports OOD robustness gains from this explicit spatial bottleneck. Relevant to [[02-projects/surf/claims/structure helps]] from the interpretability angle: if attention keypoints (geometric point locations) provide OOD robustness, it suggests structural priors over token space generalize better than end-to-end attention. Open question: are the predicted keypoints semantically grounded (object centers) or geometrically grounded (edge/boundary landmarks)?

#### Horizon

**Gaze on the Prize: Shaping Visual Attention with Return-Guided Contrastive Learning** — Lee, Chuang, Gao, Fukazawa (arXiv 2510.08442, s2_keyword)
Visual RL framework augmenting agents with a learnable foveal attention mechanism (Gaze) trained by a self-supervised contrastive objective on return-weighted image patches. The explicit use of contrastive learning to shape spatial attention toward task-relevant regions intersects [[02-projects/surf/claims/iterative selection]] and is a horizon signal for [[02-projects/surf/experiments/contrastive alignment]]: return-guided contrastive objectives could serve as an alternative training signal for SURF's visual selection module.
