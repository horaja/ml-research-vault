**core**

- [Rank Before You Encode: Reading a Few Percent of a Whole-Slide Image](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34523) — He et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Pre-encoder tile ranking for pathology: a lightweight ranker scores all tiles; only the top 2–5% are passed through the expensive foundation model encoder; accuracy is near-full-tile. Direct methodological analog of SURF's pre-encoder patch selection — validates that pre-encoder ranking is feasible without accuracy collapse, in a tile-selection setting with a comparable compute budget problem.

- [HAS: Hierarchical Adaptive Frame Sampling for Video Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34544) — Sun et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Lightweight hierarchical scorer (keyframe → clip → frame) for video MLLMs: identifies task-relevant frames before the expensive model; adaptive budget outperforms uniform sampling. Frame-level version of SURF's patch budget problem — the hierarchical coarse-to-fine scoring structure is worth comparing to SURF's spatial selector design; the adaptive (non-uniform) budget mechanism transfers directly.

- [Anytime-Valid Sequential Verification for Sensing-Efficient Robot Navigation under Perception Uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34764) — Korchi et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Applies sequential hypothesis testing (anytime-valid bounds) to decide when accumulated sensor readings suffice to safely commit to a navigation move; reduces scan budgets ~40% without safety loss. Provides a formal framework for [[02-projects/surf/claims/iterative selection]]'s when-to-stop criterion: sequential hypothesis testing on collected visual evidence could replace or formalize the uncertainty-threshold stopping rule.

**adjacent**

- [DuCo: Exploiting Dual Cross-Layer Consistency for Sparse Geometric Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34608) — Xiang et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Sparse geometric attention for visual geometry foundation models (DUSt3R-class): uses cross-layer consistency to identify attention entries that can be dropped without disrupting geometric cross-view correspondence; achieves quadratic → near-linear scaling. Relevant to SURF's sparsity-geometry tradeoff: the consistency criterion is an analog of SURF's claim that structural-prior-guided selection preserves task-relevant geometry that uniform or attention-score-based sparsity discards.

- [Most of the Way There: Turning a Strong Video Prior into an Action-Conditioned World Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34435) — Wang et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Studies which visual information must survive conversion from video prior to action-conditioned world model: semantic plausibility ≠ physical fidelity. The gap is largest where action outcomes depend on precise object geometry and contact. Directly frames SURF's core question: which visual tokens carry physical (not semantic) information needed for action accuracy, and are these the same tokens a shape prior selects?

- [HoloBrain-0.5 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34449) — Lin et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  New multi-embodiment VLA: plans in a shared action space using robot kinematic structure + calibrated camera observations; achieves strong cross-embodiment generalization. Provides a baseline: HoloBrain uses kinematic structure (a structural prior) for generalization. If SURF's V4 shape prior and HoloBrain's kinematic prior both help, they may be complementary — or one subsumes the other, which the positioning note must address.

**horizon**

- [Carrying Feature Ablations Across Sparse Autoencoder Seeds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34637) — Li · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Studies which SAE-identified vision features are reproducible across random initialization seeds: an SAE feature that disappears under re-training is an artifact, not a real encoder feature. Constrains any SAE-based interpretability analysis of SURF's target backbone: before using SAEs to characterize what the selector is selecting, need to confirm the features it supposedly activates are seed-stable.

- [Not all world models can doubt: how training objective and architecture shape epistemic uncertainty in JEPA and RSSM world models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34830) — Baur et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  JEPA (non-generative, predict-in-latent-space) produces better-calibrated epistemic uncertainty than RSSM (generative, predict-in-pixel-space) under distribution shift. Directly constrains SURF's iterative selection stopping criterion: if the VLA's underlying world model is RSSM-class, an uncertainty-based stopping rule may be miscalibrated at inference time; JEPA-class architectures are safer for uncertainty-driven adaptive budget.
