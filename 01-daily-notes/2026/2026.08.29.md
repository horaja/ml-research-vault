**core**

- [Retrieval Heads Meet Vision: Uncovering How VLMs Locate and Extract Visual Information](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27417v1) — Park et al. · arXiv Aug 2026
  Identifies "Visual Retrieval Heads" — specific attention heads responsible for locating and routing visual evidence to LLM output. Directly answers where to inject SURF's selection gate: if retrieval heads are the bottleneck for visual localization, the gate should operate around those heads rather than at the visual-encoder output layer. Read before finalizing [[experiments/contrastive alignment]] architecture section; resolves the See2Think / injection-layer open question.

- [History-Conditioned Spatio-Temporal Visual Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language Navigation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4755e2c70d9686aeb663bcb341c339a8edc6df33) — Wang et al. · arXiv Mar 2026
  Training-free token pruning for VLN using spatial clustering + temporal history of token importance; achieves 40%+ token reduction without accuracy drop. VLN (navigation), not manipulation — but shares the core problem of efficient visual token reduction under task grounding. Baseline SURF must acknowledge in the training-free section of the positioning note; temporal history conditioning is an angle SURF's contrastive prior does not currently include.

- [FlashVLA: Streaming Action Decoding for Fast and Asynchronous VLA Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27384v1) — Li et al. · arXiv Aug 2026
  Decouples VLM context encoding (slow) from flow-matching action decoding (fast) into a streaming pipeline; context is re-encoded asynchronously at lower frequency while actions decode continuously. Orthogonal efficiency axis to SURF: FlashVLA reduces *latency* via async execution; SURF reduces *visual compute* via token selection. The two compose — SURF narrows what the VLM encodes, FlashVLA lowers how often it encodes it. SURF's positioning note should acknowledge this so the compute claim is clearly about budget, not just latency.

**adjacent**

- [LeVJEPA: Efficient & Scalable Video Pretraining without the Heuristics](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/437cb869d42d536ad1b2f35399c99ab79ec65d5a) — Kuhn, Maes, Serra, Le Lidec, LeCun, Balestriero et al. · arXiv Aug 2026
  JEPA applied to video without EMA target encoders, stop-gradient, or capacity-limited predictors; uses LeJEPA's collapse-free objective. LeCun group; JEPA-family representations are patch-level predictive rather than contrastive or reconstructive. Watch this line for VLA: if V-JEPA representations are adopted in robot foundation models (as JEPA representations have been in I-JEPA → V-JEPA → potential VLA path), SURF's contrastive alignment might be redesigned around JEPA internal predictions rather than VLM attention patterns.

- [PredVLA: A Sub-Million-Parameter Predictive-Coding Policy for Robot Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53bdf4f2d06c44aa5313f039906011ef12f15044) — Sawada, Kasahara · arXiv Aug 2026
  Predictive-coding architecture (0.68M parameters) competitive with large pretrained VLAs on manipulation benchmarks. Predictive coding suppresses predictable (redundant) visual inputs at the architecture level — a different route to visual budget reduction than token selection. If temporal prediction already suppresses redundant visual content, SURF's prior may be adding structural specificity on top of a mechanism the predictive-coding baseline also captures. Skim the architecture to understand what "predicted away" means for patch-level representations.

**horizon**

- [GuidedAttention: Interpretable and Correctable Visual Attention for OOD-Robust Robot Manipulation via Imitation Learning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e21e550b5e561747284e9e919d53319b78cb9bf7) — Murooka et al. · arXiv Jul 2026
  Explicit attention keypoints predicted as an intermediate representation; human-correctable at inference; improves OOD robustness in diffusion-based manipulation policy. Orthogonal interpretability angle on SURF's "where to look" problem: SURF's gate is implicit (structural/language-driven), GuidedAttention's is explicit and correctable by hand. If SURF's structural prior produces explicit spatial attention maps, they become correctable in the same way — a free interpretability property worth noting in the contribution.

## questions

Carried forward from 2026-08-28:
- IAprune: if training-free interaction-aligned selection closes the alignment gap, what does SURF's trained contrastive prior add beyond what IAprune achieves at zero training cost?
- PACE Holistic Context Tokens: does SURF's hard patch selection suffer holistic-context loss? Should a small residual context preserve holistic signal?

- TOWN-VLA collapse: must SURF's gate operate pre-collapse?
- Sigmoid attention mismatch: should the selection gate use hard Gumbel-softmax from the start?

New today:
- Retrieval Heads Meet Vision: if specific attention heads handle visual retrieval, should SURF's gate intervene at those heads rather than at the visual-encoder output? Does this change the injection architecture assumed in the contrastive alignment spec?
- FlashVLA async decoding: SURF reduces *compute budget* (which patches are encoded); FlashVLA reduces *encoding frequency* (how often the VLM runs). Are these additive? Does FlashVLA's async model change what "efficient VLA" means as a baseline?
- PredVLA: predictive coding suppresses redundant visual patches temporally. Is SURF solving a different (structural/language-based) redundancy problem that prediction does not address? What's the experiment that shows SURF adds beyond a predictive-coding baseline?
