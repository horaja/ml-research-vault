**core**

- [History-Conditioned Spatio-Temporal Visual Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language Navigation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4755e2c70d9686aeb663bcb341c339a8edc6df33) — Wang, Liang, Li · arXiv
  Training-free spatio-temporal pruning for VLN: cross-frame history accumulates token importance scores, enabling context-aware selection that outperforms single-frame pruning. Directly validates [[claims/iterative selection]]: temporal information improves selection without additional supervision. VLN vs. manipulation is a real domain difference, but the temporal accumulation principle transfers. Check: does history conditioning add signal over a static prior, or does it substitute for one when no structural signal is available?

- [Focus-Scan-Refine: From Human Visual Perception to Efficient Visual Token Pruning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a1047da25d280a53cc064ab3877b280a707f78b0) — Tong, Bai, Zhu · arXiv
  Three-stage human-inspired token pruning: focal saliency (structural focus), scanning (spatial coverage), refinement (high-resolution re-selection). Explicit decomposition of the coarse-to-fine pipeline SURF implicitly assumes. Design question: is FSR's scanning stage equivalent to SURF's spatial coverage constraint from S²Prune? Does SURF's line-drawing prior substitute for saliency-based focusing, and if so, what does the refine stage add that the prior cannot provide?

- [Gaze on the Prize: Shaping Visual Attention with Return-Guided Contrastive Learning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac43acda376c1d2a4c79f236046064b9bb062df8) — Lee, Chuang, Gao · arXiv
  Return-signal contrastive learning shapes RL agents' visual attention; foveation-inspired. Contrastive objective with reward supervision instead of language instruction. Directly relevant to [[experiments/contrastive alignment]]: this is the baseline where contrastive alignment is grounded in return signal rather than instruction. SURF's key question: does language supervision select better task-relevant patches than return-grounded supervision, and for which task categories does instruction uniquely disambiguate (novel objects, language-specified goals, long-horizon tasks)?

**adjacent**

- [Decoupling Vision, Language, and Action for Efficient Multi-Task Robot Policies](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24eeaa684ff420a7b568fc7696de4c1b78cebd47) — Sun et al. · arXiv
  Standardized comparison of decoupled (separate vision+language encoders + compact action head) vs. monolithic VLM-backbone architectures across vision encoders and task distributions. Relevant to SURF's architecture: if decoupled encoders match full VLMs for multi-task manipulation, the selective visual compute argument shifts from latency reduction to representation quality — and SURF's structural prior becomes a feature of the vision encoder, not a patch selection layer inside the VLM.

- [Depth Matters: Language-Guided Spatial Representation Learning for Robot Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6087ec299513c92af00e2061701d128aa135681) — Zhu, Zhou, Guan · CAAI 2026
  Language-guided depth encoding as decoupled spatial representation for VLA; addresses parameter-data mismatch in VLA training on limited real-world data. Direct comparison point: depth vs. line drawings as structural signal. Depth gives metric 3D geometry; line drawings give 2D boundary/shape structure without metric scale. The comparison experiment is well-defined: same token budget, same VLA backbone, what does each prior recover and for which manipulation categories?

- [PHR-VLA: Planning Horizon Reasoning for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/360ceca3bbc857a03e40128ac8903b4b6d83d837) — Soleymanzadeh et al. · arXiv
  Conditions action prediction on future task dynamics—bridges single-step observation and long-horizon planning for fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation. Adjacent to [[claims/iterative selection]]: PHR-VLA asks what will matter in later steps; SURF asks which patches matter in this step given earlier evidence. Different questions; composable if future dynamics conditioning improves selection criteria.

**horizon**

- [Information-dependent eye-hand coordination emerges from active vision](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/439f4faa6ea6f868d768d06c98a2dcdbcc8bfc44) — Zhao, Verdel, Tan · bioRxiv
  Computational model: task-relevant gaze selects information to minimize residual uncertainty under motor planning constraints, not maximum scene saliency. This is a principled account of why task-conditioned patch selection is the right formulation for manipulation. Grounding for SURF's action-relevance axis: the correct patches are those that reduce motor-planning uncertainty—not those with maximum visual entropy or structural density.

- [Explicitly Modeling Subcortical Vision with a Neuro-Inspired Front-End Improves CNN Robustness](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d2c2818f16c45693869453594661a79b40a38dd) — Piper, Oliveira, Marques · NeurIPS
  Extends VOneBlock (V1-mimicking front-end) to include retina and LGN processing; the full retina→LGN→V1 front-end outperforms V1-only on OOD robustness. If the subcortical chain provides meaningful robustness gains over V1 alone, the computational case for early-visual priors extends beyond V1 toward V4. Horizon question: does the robustness gain transfer to spatial selection tasks, or does it saturate at V1 for recognition-type objectives?
