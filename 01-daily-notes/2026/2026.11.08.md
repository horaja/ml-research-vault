**core**

- [VLA-Tug: Failure Prediction and Classification in VLAs Via Calibrated Latent Probing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08425) — Podolinsky et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Lightweight probe over VLA backbone latent layers for runtime failure detection and failure-mode classification. Supervising on the pre-failure segment of a trajectory improves early detection by up to 30% at matched false-positive rates. Under distribution shift to unseen tasks, calibrated bounds on missed-detection and false-alarm rates hold in 86% of runs (vs. 21% for baselines). Open dataset: 3,180 annotated manipulation episodes. For SURF: the probe's effectiveness means that failure-mode information is recoverable from VLA latent representations — this is a methodology for evaluating whether SURF's structural prior changes the latent failure signature, and the dataset is a candidate evaluation set for [[claims/structure helps]] under distribution shift.

- [VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449) — Chou et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Exploits the structural regularity of visual primitives (points, bboxes, trajectories) to decode entire spatial goals jointly rather than token-by-token. 25x faster than autoregressive decoding on 3D point motion; world action model conditioned on VFlash plans achieves 70.2% vs. 62.6% success on MolmoSpaces. For SURF: parallel evidence that "structure-aware" processing outperforms sequential at multiple levels of the VLA pipeline — VFlash in the output space, SURF in the input/selection space. Both papers argue the same point: structural regularity is a resource to exploit, not a constraint to work around.

- [Shallow to Deep: Aligning Token Pruning with Stage-wise Roles in LVLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25635) — Zhang et al. · arXiv Sep 2026
  Finds that training-free attention-based pruning in shallow ViT layers consistently hurts performance because shallow layers function primarily as edge detectors with chaotic attention patterns, not semantic selectors. Proposes STD: high-frequency spectral analysis in shallow layers to preserve structural edges, Gaussian-smoothed attention in intermediate layers for spatial coherence, and a stability-adaptive trigger in deep layers for semantic pruning. Achieves 1.1% gain over prior pruning at 88.9% token reduction on LLaVA-1.5-7B; 2.1% gain at 94.4% reduction with 3.9x prefilling speedup on LLaVA-NeXT-7B. For SURF: this is the most direct empirical answer to the stage-placement question (open since Nov 5, day 3). The finding that shallow layers are edge detectors with chaotic attention is consistent with SURF's line-drawing prior operating at the pre-encoder level — structural edges must be preserved before deep semantic pruning is safe. STD's shallow-layer spectral analysis (keeping high-frequency structural content) is structurally analogous to SURF's line-drawing selection. The comparison question: does SURF's explicit structural prior at pre-encoder level select the same tokens that STD's spectral analysis preserves?

- [History-Conditioned Spatio-Temporal Visual Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06480) — Wang et al. · arXiv Mar 2026
  Training-free plug-and-play token pruning for VLA-based Vision-Language Navigation: selects spatially important tokens from the current view using attention-based importance scoring, and compresses historical visual memory using query-guided filtering across timesteps. Competitive navigation accuracy under aggressive pruning; demonstrates low-latency instruction following on a Unitree Go2 quadruped. For SURF: the most direct training-free spatial pruning baseline for VLA navigation — closest to SURF's operational regime (inference-time, no retraining, structural selection). The query-guided temporal filtering is adjacent to SURF's language-conditioning claim ([[claims/language helps]]): both use the instruction query to gate which tokens survive. Key comparison: SURF's static single-frame structural prior (line-drawing) vs. this paper's dynamic history-conditioned selection — does incorporating temporal structure change which tokens the structural prior selects?

**adjacent**

- [Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08458) — Wang et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Distills large-scale RL rollouts (privileged simulation) into latent priors for visual policy learning; achieves data-efficient manipulation from limited demonstrations. Alternative to SURF's approach: SURF injects structural priors at the visual token-selection level before the LLM; this paper injects control priors at the latent representation level before action decoding. The comparison axis for the positioning note: pre-encoder selection (SURF) vs. post-encoder latent priors (this paper) — both address the data-efficiency problem but at different pipeline stages.

- [Learning the Task or Learning the Verifier? Rethinking Criteria Exposure in Embodied Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08318) — Huang et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Many recent embodied self-improvement papers expose benchmark success criteria to the agent; this inflates reported success because criteria demand more than the task instruction. Directly impacts how SURF's evaluation should be designed: success on LIBERO or similar benchmarks may reflect criteria-learning rather than genuine structural-prior benefit. The paper introduces BenchCE to audit this exposure. Read before finalizing SURF's evaluation plan — ensure SURF's metrics are genuinely task-completion signals, not criteria-shaped proxies.

**horizon**

- [First-Token Attraction in Mamba Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08303) — Nguyen et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Studies the "first-token sink" phenomenon in deep Mamba models: a first-token attracting mechanism creates token dynamics that differ structurally from attention-based sinks. NEO-VLA uses a Mamba-Transformer hybrid (from Nov 5). If VLAs shift toward encoder-free Mamba architectures, SURF's structural prior operates in a system where token dynamics are governed by Mamba's matrix-valued interactions rather than attention. The first-token sink may interact with or override structural patch selection — worth checking against NEO-VLA's design before writing the positioning note.
