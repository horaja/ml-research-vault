Saturday, August 15. Contrastive alignment spec unwritten — 30 weeks. GSPA unread — day 64. "When Attention Collapses" STS unread — day 52. Visual Access Boundaries unread — day 32. AtVLA unread — day 14.

Today's most important find is **Beyond Attention or Similarity: Maximizing Conditional Diversity for Token Pruning in MLLMs** (Zhang et al., 2025-06-12, 83 citations) — a high-impact paper that the digest has not surfaced until now. It reformulates instruction-conditioned token selection as a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) problem: select the most conditionally diverse visual token subset given the instruction, beyond simple attention-score or similarity thresholds. At 83 citations, this is canonical prior work surf must cite and differentiate from. The DPP criterion predicts that pure language-attention selection (SparseVLM+) captures instruction-salient but redundant patches, while surf's structural geometry prior may add *diversity* specifically around object boundaries and contact surfaces — exactly where manipulation affordances live. This distinction should be the spec's related-work framing for [[claims/structure helps]] vs. language-only baselines.

A second paper: **RoboPCA** (Xiao et al., 2026-03-08) jointly predicts task-appropriate contact regions and contact poses from instructions using demonstrations. This is a direct evaluation target for surf: IoU between line-density top-k patch selection and RoboPCA's contact region predictions on the same manipulation frames would test whether structural geometry recovers affordance-relevant patches without demonstrations — directly grounding [[claims/structure helps]].

**adjacent**

- [FocusUI: Efficient UI Grounding via Position-Preserving Visual Token Selection](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/022a6b853a918ddaa7201b40e48d566fc797ed16) — Ouyang et al. · S2 2026-01-07
  Visual token selection for UI grounding with PosPad: compresses each dropped-token sequence into a single positional marker at the last dropped index, preserving positional continuity for the downstream grounding head. Direct architectural design reference for surf: when surf drops patches by line density, the downstream VLA's position embeddings become non-contiguous. - [DreamX-Phi 1.0: Action-Conditioned Video World Model for Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13489v1) — DreamX Team · arXiv 2026-08-13
  Predicts future visual observations from an observed frame, language instruction, and prescribed action sequence (end-effector poses + gripper state). Relevant to surf as a potential training signal source: tokens that, when dropped, increase world-model prediction error on action-conditioned future frames would identify action-relevant patches empirically — a supervision signal for the contrastive scorer that doesn't require gaze data. Competing with TEMPO's frozen-backbone constraint: whether scorer training from world-model gradients disrupts the VLM backbone is the key architectural question.

**horizon**

- [Contrastive Learning via Variational Information Bottleneck](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a715468d022c3cf3fa87f1c72d6809f6c266444) — Li et al. · S2 2025-05-20 · 10 citations
  Shows that self-supervised contrastive learning (maximizing mutual information between augmentations) is equivalent to variational information bottleneck maximization under certain conditions. Theoretical grounding for surf's contrastive scorer design: the IB framing makes the optimization explicit — maximize information about task-relevant patches, minimize information about irrelevant ones — and suggests a rate-distortion tradeoff governing the optimal patch count. If surf's contrastive objective is interpretable as IB maximization, the budget parameter k corresponds to a rate constraint, and the IB literature's results on sufficient statistics apply to patch selection.

## questions

- Beyond Attention or Similarity × spec design: DPP selects conditionally diverse tokens given instruction — it captures *coverage* of instruction-relevant visual regions. Surf's structural prior selects by line density — it captures *geometric informativeness*. Are these complementary (DPP + structural geometry outperforms either alone), competing (one subsumes the other on manipulation frames), or orthogonal (DPP excels at instruction-disambiguation, structural geometry at affordance localization)? The spec ablation needs a DPP baseline: run Zhang et al.'s DPP criterion on LIBERO frames and compare contact-region IoU against line-density selection and the full surf selector.
- RoboPCA × [[claims/structure helps]] evaluation: RoboPCA provides instruction-conditioned contact region predictions from demonstrations. If line-density top-k patches achieve high IoU with RoboPCA's contact masks on unseen objects, structural geometry recovers affordance regions without demonstrations — the core training-free claim. If IoU is low, structural priors miss contact regions that require task-specific shape understanding, and surf needs a learned component. Is RoboPCA's contact region definition compatible with surf's patch grid, or are contact regions too fine-grained for ViT patch resolution?
- PatchGen × [[claims/structure helps]] generalization: If line-density-selected patches constitute the predictive sufficient subgraph for in-distribution manipulation frames, does the same selection remain sufficient under OOD scenes (different lighting, viewpoint, object instances)? PatchGen's framework predicts that a properly adaptive selection should remain sufficient; line-density's non-adaptive nature means it may select the wrong sufficient subgraph in OOD scenes. This is the core generalization failure mode to test.
- DreamX-Phi × contrastive scorer training: If future-observation prediction error is highest for action-relevant tokens (those that change as the end-effector moves), dropping these tokens hurts prediction accuracy. Can this prediction-error signal replace or complement gaze supervision for training surf's contrastive scorer? The benefit: no gaze data needed. The risk: TEMPO (day 6) shows that world-model gradients through a VLM backbone can disrupt pretrained representations.
- FocusUI × position-preserving selection: PosPad places a marker at the last dropped index per contiguous dropped sequence. Surf's line-density selection is not necessarily contiguous — high-density patches may be scattered. Does PosPad generalize to non-contiguous drop patterns, or does it require contiguous token segments? If non-contiguous, a different positional compression mechanism is needed for surf's architecture.
