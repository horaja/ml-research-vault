- [S²Prune: Spatially Structured Visual Token Pruning for Multimodal Large Language Models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/613f2d55a193eb6ffbb688204eb4fe6bfeb1ec72) — Jia, Tang, Yang · S2 recommend
  Importance and redundancy criteria produce spatial biases across inputs and do not outperform Uniform Grid sampling; S²Prune fixes this with spatial coverage (≥1 token/region) + Laplacian variation budget allocation + ERC within-region selection. The threat to [[claims/structure helps]] is confirmed in static MLLM settings. SURF's differentiation question sharpens: does the line-drawing prior recover task-specific layout (object contact geometry, reachable workspace structure) or is it mainly a proxy for local Laplacian variance? These are measurable and different.

- [GSPA: Geo-Semantic Prior-Guided Attention for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/593fe3f7bbdcbd6169f9903fcab28e8e65316c4e) — Che et al. · ICSI 2026
  Geo-semantic prior-guided attention for long-horizon VLA manipulation under clutter, contact dynamics, and viewpoint variation. Day 100 unread. The "geo" component remains unresolved — whether it is depth, edge maps, or learned geometry determines whether GSPA is a competing method or a complementary baseline. The long-horizon framing (stage transition + consistent visual grounding across rollout) is directly relevant to SURF's iterative selection claim. Required read for positioning note.

- [Attention from Action, for Action: Emergent Visual Bottlenecks for Policy Learning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/97c2db623247367a7622c4c8b0c79bb41f098dcc) — Zhuang, Wang, Heppert, Valada, Pokorny, Kragic · S2 keyword
  Proposes emergent visual bottlenecks derived from action policy rollouts (gripper/motion event detection) without external spatial annotations. Label-free ROI derivation from trajectory dynamics. Directly defines the key axis: SURF's line-drawing geometry requires no robot data; this approach requires trajectory rollouts. The data-free vs. trajectory-derived structural prior comparison is the experiment that distinguishes them — equivalent token budget, same VLA backbone, what does each prior recover and at what data cost?

**adjacent**

- [SAVLA: Symmetry-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models for Robotic Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d1f93e6c5590ad9a52d8908f356fd1f264dcb0f5) — Li et al. · S2 recommend
  Injects geometric symmetry (equivariance) as a structural inductive bias into VLA to improve OOD generalization beyond the demonstration pose range. Different geometric primitive from SURF (equivariance vs. patch selection / line-drawing coverage), but the same motivation: geometry improves OOD robustness. The comparison question: does equivariance capture the same OOD benefit as task-conditioned structural patch selection, or are they orthogonal (equivariance handles viewpoint shifts; structural selection handles scene clutter and irrelevant regions)?

- [GuidedAttention: Interpretable and Correctable Visual Attention for OOD-Robust Robot Manipulation via Imitation Learning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e21e550b5e561747284e9e919d53319b78cb9bf7) — Murooka, Nakajo et al. · arXiv
  Predicts task-relevant attention keypoints as an explicit intermediate representation that conditions a diffusion policy; keypoints are human-correctable for OOD handling. Keypoint-level vs. patch-level spatial representation is the key design difference from SURF. SURF's structural prior operates before inference; GuidedAttention uses keypoints as a trained intermediate. Relevant to [[claims/structure helps]] from the OOD angle: if explicit keypoints outperform structural patch selection on OOD, the patch-level abstraction needs justification.

- Gaze on the Prize: structural geometry vs. return-guided contrastive (no prior) OOD?
- History-Conditioned VT Pruning: cross-step history conditioning vs. within-step structural geometry — complementary or substitutable?

New from today:
- Attention from Action, for Action: do emergent action-policy bottlenecks select the same patches as SURF's structural prior? If yes, the prior is an implicit action proxy; if no, which one correlates better with task success on held-out tasks?
- SAVLA: is the OOD gain from symmetry (equivariance) orthogonal to the OOD gain from structural patch selection? Could both be composed in a single VLA?
- GuidedAttention: for OOD recovery, does patch-level structural selection (SURF) or keypoint-level annotation-able attention (GuidedAttention) provide a better inductive bias? Under what conditions does human correctability matter vs. fully automatic structure?
