**core**

- [FoAViS: Analyzing Learned Fixation Strategies in a Foveated Active Vision System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41987) — Hassler et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Implements a machine system with human-like foveated active vision (high-res fovea, peripheral falloff) and analyzes which fixation strategies emerge from training objectives. The controlled setting — known components, known objectives — allows systematic study of gaze behavior that may inform understanding of biological vision. SURF needs exactly this kind of ablatable active-perception system to evaluate structural prior quality against a learned fixation baseline; this paper may serve as an implementation reference or a directly comparable architecture.

- [RTPolicy: Real-Time (32.9 Hz) Multi-View Visuomotor Policy for Robotic Manipulation on Edge Devices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41625) — Hou et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Achieves 32.9 Hz closed-loop manipulation by addressing the visual backbone efficiency bottleneck in VLA policies. Read to extract the specific efficiency mechanism — if it uses token dropping, backbone compression, or patch selection — and determine whether it constitutes a compute-matched baseline SURF must beat. The frequency claim (32.9 Hz) sets a concrete latency floor for [[claims/efficiency without collapse]] in action tasks.

- [A Patch is More Than a Token](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41814) — Wang & Ma · arXiv Oct 2026
  Argues a patch's representation is not a single canonical token but a "Representation State Family" under identity-preserving variation; introduces patch-level local feature space grounded in this observation. Theoretical support for SURF's implicit assumption: structural patch selection is selecting over a richer object than a single token index, meaning patch-level priors operate in a higher-fidelity representation space than attention-score token-importance rankings treat. Check whether the framework makes any predictions about which patch variations (structural edge content vs. appearance texture) are identity-preserving vs. identity-altering.

**adjacent**

- [When Does Test-Time Search Help World Action Models? An Empirical Audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41970) — Liu et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  On clean data, a learned value selector performs 1.66 pp *worse* than random on RoboCasa; hindsight oracle shows there is headroom, but standard selectors can't exploit it. Direct challenge to SURF's adaptive compute framing: if selective compute at inference time fails to beat random selection even with a trained selector, SURF must either show its structural prior is categorically different (deterministic, training-free structure) or identify why the value-selector failure mode doesn't apply. Critical to read before finalizing the [[experiments/contrastive alignment]] evaluation design.

- [Attention Maps Know When to Think: Training-Free Difficulty-Aware CoT for Reasoning Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41618) — Cho et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Attention map entropy predicts per-sample reasoning difficulty for visual grounding; uses entropy to decide when to invoke CoT vs. skip it. Directly relevant to [[claims/iterative selection]]: if attention entropy is a reliable difficulty signal for spatial tasks, SURF can use the same signal to trigger refinement passes — "refine when the prior is uncertain" operationalized via attention distribution spread. Read to check whether the entropy signal is patch-level or token-level, and whether it degrades under distribution shift.

- [REA: A Routed Embodied Agent Runtime for Asynchronous Brain-Cerebellum Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42053) — Zhou et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Asynchronous runtime coupling a deliberative foundation model (brain) with a fast reactive VLA (cerebellum). The routing mechanism decides when to invoke the slow path vs. execute the cached fast path. Adjacent to SURF's three-module architecture: if SURF's structural prior module is a fast pre-selection step and the VLA is the slow downstream, this paper's routing and timing analysis may inform how the prior and VLA interact under latency constraints.

**horizon**

- [Hierarchical World Models with Implicit Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42146) — Zhou et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Decouples state-space reachability from latent transition dynamics in a hierarchical world model; avoids brittle latent skill representations. Relevant for understanding where SURF's visual selection sits in a longer-horizon planning stack: if world models handle reachability at the macro scale and VLA handles execution, structural visual token selection may operate only in the execution layer — meaning SURF's efficiency claim is bounded by VLA inference cost, not full planning cost.
