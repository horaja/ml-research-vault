**core**

- [Beyond the Sink Interpretation: Radial Attention in Flow-Based VLA Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26385) — Zheng, Lin, Liu et al. · arXiv
  Tests whether first/last action tokens in flow-based VLA (π₀.₅) are true attention sinks (high incoming attention + small values + negligible output effect) or structurally important tokens. Directly relevant to SURF's pre-encoder selection rationale: if VLA radial attention is NOT a sink pattern but a signal of action-relevant token importance, selecting tokens that drive radial attention could inform where the structural prior should focus. Read before the positioning note's VLA attention mechanism section.

- [A²-VLA: Adapting Vision-Language-Action Policies to New Tools through Agentic Affordance Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25656) — Zhou, Huang, Wang et al. · arXiv
  Discovers common affordances across tools with different shapes to enable skill transfer: tool geometry encodes task-relevant function. Supporting evidence for [[02-projects/surf/claims/structure helps]] — if geometric shape is the carrier of manipulation affordance across tool variations, then a shape-based prior (V4 structural selectivity) is encoding the right information for action relevance. The question for SURF: does the learned affordance representation overlap with what a V4 shape prior selects on tabletop manipulation scenes?

**adjacent**

- [Rank2Replace: VLM-Ranked Slot Replacement for Out-of-Distribution Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25834) — Feng, Liao, Lin et al. · arXiv
  Uses a pretrained VLM to rank task-relevant vs. spurious object slots and replace spurious slots during training augmentation to improve OOD accuracy. Adjacent to SURF's shortcut robustness angle: both approaches use structured prior knowledge to suppress spurious correlations, but Rank2Replace operates at training time via augmentation while SURF's structural prior operates at inference via pre-encoder selection. Useful as a comparison baseline for the "shortcut suppression" claim.

- [Reasoning on Call: Efficient Agentic Robot Control for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26341) — Cen, Tang, Liu et al. · arXiv
  Determines when to invoke the expensive multimodal model vs. use a reactive policy for long-horizon robot manipulation. This is macro-level adaptive compute budgeting — when to call the model — adjacent to SURF's micro-level adaptive visual budget — what to process within a call. The two levels are orthogonal and potentially composable: SURF's token selection reduces the cost of each call; RoC reduces the number of calls. Worth monitoring as a complementary efficiency axis.

- [PhyDEX: A Benchmark for Physical Reasoning in VLA via Dynamic Extrinsic Dexterity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26565) — Vo, Hanyu, Ikebe et al. · arXiv
  Benchmarks VLA physics reasoning: gravity, friction, inertia, mass distribution. Standard benchmarks only test gripper-object contact; PhyDEX exposes whether VLA policies understand physical consequences of manipulation. Relevant to SURF's eval design: tasks requiring physics understanding likely demand scene structural information (object geometry, contact geometry) that V4 shape priors encode. A strong potential eval complement to LIBERO-style benchmarks.

**horizon**

- [HiSA: Sparsifying the Hidden Dimension of RoPE Attention in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26363) — Borbely, Salzmann, Verscheure et al. · arXiv
  Proposes Hidden Sparse Attention: computes only a partial dot product in RoPE attention, following a static position-based rule (not learned). Parameter-free, O(1) overhead. Horizon-tier efficiency insight: if attention can be approximated with a partial hidden-dimension dot product via positional rules, this opens a path to position-structured sparsity complementary to SURF's token-level selection. The positional structure of the sparsity rule is analogous to SURF's structural prior — static spatial structure as an approximation signal.
