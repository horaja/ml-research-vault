**core**

- [Model-Free Runtime Measurement of Task-Relevant Information](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14111) — Panagopoulos et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Proposes a model-free method to monitor at runtime whether autonomous systems are acquiring task-relevant information during the information-gathering process — no ground-truth task labels required. Measures information density against task completion signals. For SURF: a direct evaluation primitive for the task-relevant information acquisition question. Applied to SURF's selected token subset, this framework could independently verify whether structurally selected tokens carry higher task-relevant information — separating selection quality from downstream action performance. Pairs with ACTSB (Nov 9) for SURF's eval design.

**adjacent**

- [From Video Frames to Robot Meta-Frames: A Unified Latent Interface for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14365) — Liang et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Introduces "meta-frames" that inject low-dimensional robot signals (proprioception, actions, values) into the latent space of pretrained video diffusion models for World Action Models (WAMs). The key design: robot state is encoded at the same resolution as video frames and merged in the VDM latent space. For SURF: the meta-frame interface defines where robot signals and visual features interact. If SURF's structural token selection operates pre-encoder, the meta-frame merging happens after selection — SURF's structural prior must remain informative about task-relevant regions even after robot signal injection in the latent.

- [Right for the Wrong Reasons? Evaluating Plausibility and Behavioral Change in Explanation-Guided Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14134) — Dembinsky et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Finds that improved alignment between model explanations and human-provided rationales does not reliably produce behavioral change: models can achieve high plausibility scores while maintaining unchanged decision strategies. Studies the plausibility-behavior gap under controlled conditions. For SURF: an eval design threat to [[02-projects/surf/claims/structure helps]]. If SURF's structural prior constrains visual attention to task-relevant regions but the action pipeline adapts without using those regions differently, SURF's selection may be plausible but behaviorally null. The decomposition of plausibility vs. behavioral change provides a template for SURF's eval design.

- [OnlyAct: Extracting Action Representations from Foundation Models for World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14420) — Li et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Extracts action representations from large vision-language foundation models without explicit action labels, via cross-modal alignment between video features and implicit action signals. For SURF: the cross-modal alignment mechanism parallels SURF's contrastive alignment objective. If action-relevant visual features are latent in pretrained VLM representations and can be extracted without task-specific annotations, this constrains how much of SURF's contrastive training is adding new signal vs. surfacing already-latent representations.

**horizon**

- [Measuring Robustness and Efficiency in a Connectome-Constrained Fly Visual System Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14498) — Garcia-Duran et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Tests whether a Drosophila connectome-constrained visual model achieves greater robustness at lower computational cost than unconstrained networks. Finds adversarial robustness and susceptibility to perceptual illusions are coupled in the bio-constrained model. For SURF: the most direct empirical test of the "structural wiring constraints → robustness + efficiency" hypothesis in a biological visual system. If connectome-constrained wiring produces robustness-efficiency coupling in fly vision, this supports SURF's claim that structural priors (line drawings ~ edge-detection wiring) improve both selectivity and robustness in manipulation VLAs.
