# 2026-10-08

## opener

Positioning note is day 44. No new structural blockers added today. The language-conditioning axis now has two new stress tests that must inform the contrastive alignment spec before writing.

"Rephrase Before You Act" lands today as a direct empirical challenge to [[claims/language helps]]: VLAs show instruction-phrasing sensitivity of up to 61 pp success swing from a single-word edit, and this sensitivity persists even with rephrase augmentation. SURF's contrastive prior scores patches by language-patch compatibility — if the backbone's language representation is this fragile, the patch-language similarity signal used for contrastive scoring inherits the same instability. The question is whether structural spatial selection is upstream of the language fragility (i.e., geometric patches are selected before language conditioning corrupts the signal) or downstream (i.e., the contrastive score itself is polluted by phrasing noise). This is now a required analysis before the contrastive alignment spec is finalized.

"Do VLA Models Understand Instructions?" (Wulff et al.) applies activation and attribution patching to π₀.₅ and GR00T N1.7 to probe whether action generation actually depends on language instructions. If language grounding in VLA backbones is shallow, SURF's contrastive prior may be scoring patches against a near-meaningless signal. Read alongside "Rephrase Before You Act" before the contrastive alignment spec.

COSTGRAD (Xu & Grefenstette) is today's competitive threat to SURF's efficiency module: a training-free token selector for world model planning that ranks tokens by the gradient of the planning cost w.r.t. each input token, achieving 2.6× speedup at 50% sparsity. This is the first published method that explicitly selects tokens by "what matters for planning, not prediction" — the same claim SURF's task-relevant structural prior is supposed to make. SURF must either (a) demonstrate it selects the same tokens as COSTGRAD without online gradient computation, or (b) argue that structural priors generalize to new tasks while COSTGRAD requires running the world model.

Cao et al. is day 9. Still unread.

- [Rephrase Before You Act: Characterizing and Mitigating Language Sensitivity in Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10526v1) — Watts et al. · arXiv

Critical question for the contrastive alignment spec: does structural patch pre-selection happen upstream of the language sensitivity (geometric structure is instruction-independent) or is the contrastive score itself corrupted by phrasing variance?

- [Do Vision-Language-Action Models Understand Instructions? A Mechanistic Interpretability Study on Language Grounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10178v1) — Wulff & Cangelosi · arXiv
  Activation and attribution patching on π₀.₅ and GR00T N1.7 probes whether action generation actually uses language instructions or relies on visual cues and superficial correlations. Mechanistic grounding for [[claims/language helps]]: if VLA language grounding is shallow, language-conditioned patch scoring gains nothing over vision-only selection, and SURF's structural prior alone would be the correct baseline. Read before finalizing the contrastive alignment spec.

- [Sparse Planning in Visual World Models via Cost Gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10274v1) — Xu & Grefenstette · arXiv
  COSTGRAD: training-free spatial token selector that ranks tokens by gradient norm of the planning cost w.r.t. each input token; matches or exceeds full-token planning at 50% sparsity on 3/4 continuous-control benchmarks, 2.6× speedup. Selects "tokens that matter for planning rather than prediction" — exactly SURF's task-relevant selection claim. First published competitor that explicitly optimizes for downstream control objective rather than reconstruction quality. SURF's structural prior must beat or distinguish from COSTGRAD: zero-shot geometric selection (no world model needed) vs. online cost-gradient computation (requires a deployed world model but is task-ground-truth-guided).

**adjacent**

- [Explicit Geometric Chain-of-Thought for Vision-Language-Action in Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10390v1) — Gui et al. · arXiv
  GeoCoTDrive: "think with 2D first, drive with 3D priors" — 2D semantic regions identified before 3D geometric cues are injected for planning. Different domain (driving) but the 2D→3D decomposition and the diagnosis that 2D VL reasoning is insufficient for precise action mirror SURF's claim structure. Check: does the 2D pre-processing stage in GeoCoTDrive align with the kind of structural token selection SURF proposes at the input stage?

- [QuadTok: Quadtree Visual Tokenizer for Autoregressive Image Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10497v1) — Mao et al. · arXiv
  Hierarchical quadtree tokenization allocates representational capacity to visually complex regions while leaving homogeneous areas at coarse resolution; saves ~10% tokens vs. fixed 256-token grid on ImageNet. Different task (autoregressive image gen), but the "allocate more capacity to intricate areas" principle mirrors SURF's adaptive visual budget. Key check: does quadtree complexity (spatial gradient, texture change) correlate with SURF's line-drawing-derived structural selection in manipulation scenes?

**horizon**

- [RoboJEPA: Scaling Robotic Latent World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10515v1) — Zholus et al. · arXiv

The scaling law finding is also a reference point: if downstream task performance follows a power law, SURF's token budget interacts predictably with the scaling curve.

## questions

New from today:
- Rephrase Before You Act: Does SURF's structural prior select patches in an instruction-independent way, meaning the contrastive patch-language scoring happens after a geometry-gated token subset is already fixed? If yes, phrasing sensitivity affects only the contrastive scoring step, not the input-stage selection. If no, language-conditioned patch selection in SURF is vulnerable to the same phrasing-induced variance.
- Rephrase Before You Act: Does rephrase augmentation at training time reduce phrasing sensitivity in the contrastive prior as well, or does the VLA backbone's residual sensitivity persist through the patch-scoring pathway?
- VLA Interpretability (Wulff): What fraction of VLA action variance is explained by language vs. visual attribution? If language attribution is near-zero on most manipulation episodes, [[claims/language helps]] requires either a domain-specific argument (e.g., spatial reference disambiguation) or a substantial reframing.
- COSTGRAD: Does COSTGRAD's cost-gradient-based token importance signal correlate with SURF's structural-prior-based selection on manipulation scenes? If they select the same tokens, structural priors are an efficient zero-shot approximation to COSTGRAD; if they diverge, SURF's prior selects structurally-important tokens that COSTGRAD misses.
- COSTGRAD: COSTGRAD requires an online world model; SURF's prior is input-stage and training-free. Under what manipulation conditions does SURF's zero-shot geometric prior match COSTGRAD's quality, and when does it fail?
