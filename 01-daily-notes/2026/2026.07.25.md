#### Core

**GuidedAttention** (Murooka et al., arXiv:2607.21049) — Visuomotor imitation framework that predicts task-relevant attention keypoints as an explicit intermediate representation and conditions a diffusion-based action policy on them; keypoints are inspectable and user-correctable at rollout initialization, with a tracking module propagating the correction through execution. Directly parallel to surf's prior module: the diagnostic question is whether GuidedAttention's learned keypoints fall on the same contact-geometry patches as surf's line-density prior — co-location positive would confirm that structural prior selection and learned attention selection converge at contact events.

**VLAFlow** (Xia et al., arXiv:2607.01586) — Controlled comparison of four VLA training objectives (action-only, language-supervised, future-latent, combined) under fixed pi0-style architecture and shared OXEMix data on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, SimplerEnv: language supervision preserves vision-language generalization; future latent alignment improves state-transition modeling; combining both is the most stable transfer. Directly sets the LIBERO baseline for [[claims/language helps]] — what language supervision alone gains before structural prior is added, informing the ablation table in [[experiments/contrastive alignment]].

**SARL** (Bhatia, Wagenmaker, Chen, Levine, arXiv:2606.31958) — Adapts a frozen generalist VLA policy by optimizing in language prompt space via online RL, treating prompts as a controllable skill parametrization rather than a passive conditioning signal; solves long-horizon manipulation tasks that zero-shot VLA cannot. Strong version of the Compressor-VLA hypothesis: if prompt-space RL fully parametrizes the policy's skill repertoire on LIBERO-class tasks without structural prior, the go/no-go question for surf narrows to whether contact-geometry patches remain unreachable through instruction conditioning alone.

#### Adjacent

**S²-VLA** (Xie et al., arXiv:2606.27872) — 2B VLA with state-space guided adaptive attention that maintains a belief state over task progression and generates dynamic gating weights to fuse visual, language, and action features; SOTA on LIBERO long-horizon tasks and SimplerEnv, outperforming 7B-scale models. Adaptive visual selection via belief state rather than structural prior — clean adaptive-fusion competitor with the same efficiency motivation as surf.

**ActionCache** (Oi et al., arXiv:2607.06370) — Training-free plug-in cache for flow-matching VLAs that retrieves intermediate action representations from past similar contexts using compact multimodal keys, achieving 11.75× acceleration on π0.5 and 34.43× on GR00T with maintained success rates in simulation and real-world. Competing efficiency path to surf: caching past actions vs. reducing visual input tokens — both target deployment latency from opposite directions.

**DiMaS** (Khayatan et al., arXiv:2607.14280) — Distribution-matching steering for flow-matching VLAs; shows behavioral features in the action expert are linearly decodable but not linearly steerable, motivating optimal-transport-based distribution matching between representation distributions rather than linear direction shifts. Constrains [[experiments/contrastive alignment]]: if action-expert representations are not linearly steerable, a contrastive scorer that assumes a linear scoring function may require nonlinear parameterization.

#### Horizon

**How are linear representations learned?** (Yang, Saxe, Latham, arXiv:2607.08843) — Exact dynamical theory of abstraction in neural networks: data and target geometry jointly determine end-of-learning representation alignment; depth improves abstraction; initialization scale controls the maximum reached during training; an attenuation law holds for nonlinear networks (erf, ReLU), verified in DINOv3 and Gemma 4. Theoretical grounding for when surf's line-drawing prior — which biases toward a specific feature geometry — will persist through VLM training dynamics vs. be attenuated.
