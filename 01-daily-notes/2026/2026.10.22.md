# 2026-10-22

## opener

Positioning note is day 58. The unread queue remains the bottleneck: Cao et al. (day 23), TLC-DiT, SAPrune, SPAR.

Today's digest adds a new structural thread. **GeoMem-VLA** proposes accumulating 3D geometric structure across timesteps into a persistent temporal memory, rather than inferring it per-frame and discarding it. This is a competing architecture to SURF's prior module: where SURF applies a 2D structural prior to select patches at each frame, GeoMem-VLA argues that 2D per-frame geometric structure is insufficient and temporal accumulation is required. The claim depends on the task regime — for novel-object manipulation where 3D geometry matters (contact regions, object extent), GeoMem-VLA's temporal 3D memory may cover what SURF's 2D line-drawing prior misses. The question for positioning: does SURF's 2D structural prior capture a different aspect of geometry (contour/shape) than depth-based 3D memory (distance/occupancy), or are they redundant on manipulation scenes?

**Watch Your Step** provides a clean locomotion parallel to SURF's core problem: only the terrain beneath future footsteps informs the control decision, but learned encoders process the full elevation map. They solve this by predicting which terrain regions will matter before the policy acts ("anticipated perception"). Same structural move as SURF's prior module — pre-select what is visually relevant before the action model runs. The locomotion framing may be useful for positioning SURF's contribution in a broader active-perception frame.

**When Text Matters** is directly relevant to [[experiments/contrastive alignment]]: image-based token selection overlooks task-relevant details; text-guided selection may fail to capture text-visual alignment. Their design principles constrain the hybrid text+structure selector SURF is designing.

Read SAPrune today (stage-placement decision) before writing the contrastive alignment spec.

- [GeoMem-VLA: Internalizing 3D Scene Understanding into Temporal Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29866v1) — Zhang et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Accumulates 3D geometric structure into a persistent temporal memory rather than inferring geometry per-frame and discarding it; achieves stronger manipulation performance on tasks requiring spatial continuity. Competing architecture to SURF's prior module: where SURF applies a 2D structural prior per-frame, GeoMem-VLA argues 3D temporal accumulation is the correct level. Check whether SURF's 2D line-drawing prior (contour/shape) is redundant with their depth-based memory (occupancy/distance) or complementary on novel-object manipulation scenes. Required for positioning note's geometric-prior axis.

- [Watch Your Step: Anticipated Perception for Humanoid Locomotion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29612v1) — Heyrman et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Pre-selects which terrain regions will be relevant for upcoming footsteps before the locomotion policy runs; shows that learning "where to attend" separately from locomotion speeds training and avoids encoding irrelevant scene context. Structural parallel to SURF's prior module — both solve the same problem (pre-select visual tokens before the action model) in different embodiment regimes. The anticipated-perception framing may strengthen SURF's positioning: the prior module is not a compression technique but an anticipation mechanism.

- [When Text Matters: Design Principles for Visual Token Pruning in Vision-Language Models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e916b0358f6d215227c6296dcf5c84e0ed06e60e) — Kang et al. · arXiv
  Studies failure modes of image-only vs. text-guided visual token pruning: image-only overlooks task-relevant regions; text-guided fails when text-visual alignment is absent for novel objects. Derives design principles for hybrid selection. Directly constrains [[experiments/contrastive alignment]] spec — the "text-guided alone fails for novel objects" finding is exactly the regime SURF's geometric prior is designed to cover. Read before finalizing the contrastive alignment objective.

**adjacent**

- [Later Is Better: Token Reduction for ViTs Under Distribution Shift](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/681a0f38d181683ac11abec27ba11232dd285780) — Cha et al. · arXiv
  Training-free semantic token reduction degrades faster under distribution shift than on clean data; the gap widens with removal rate. Adjacent to [[claims/structure helps]] at the affordance-evaluation level: if part-level affordance regions are the ground truth for what SURF's prior should select, UniRAP's output is a potential evaluation target. **horizon**

- [JEPA Learns What the Mask Leaves Unrecoverable](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61eec504ce5432e35b648e6f26588cd1d7b5eb70) — Xie et al. · arXiv
  Measurement-theoretic account of why block masks work in JEPA/MAE and scattered masks fail: a block mask places low-frequency wavelet atoms entirely inside the hidden region, making the local structure unrecoverable and forcing the model to learn structure rather than noise statistics. Horizon for [[eval/reconstruction]]: if SURF's structural prior selects patches that correspond to structurally recoverable regions, the MAE reconstruction metric may be testing the wrong thing — structural selection should leave structurally irrelevant regions masked, not vice versa. Also relevant to why line-drawing-guided block selection is compatible with MAE-style pre-training.
