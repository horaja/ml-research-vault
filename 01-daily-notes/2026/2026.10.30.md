## opener

Positioning note is day 66. Unread queue unchanged: Attention from Action, SAPrune, TLC-DiT, SPAR, Cao et al., Shi et al. Stage-Aware. Every downstream spec decision remains blocked on that first read.

Two themes emerge from today's digest. First, architectural validation: Watch Your Step (Heyrman et al.) deploys a pre-selection prior in a real humanoid system — an "anticipator" module selects terrain patches around predicted footsteps before the locomotion policy encoder runs. The design decision — select spatially relevant patches before the policy backbone, train the selector separately — is a direct real-system instantiation of SURF's prior module framing. The selector is temporal (predicted footsteps) rather than structural (line drawings), but the architecture and the training decoupling are exact matches. Second, evaluation data: GazeAct (Pani et al.) provides real human gaze paired with multi-camera manipulation observations and language instructions, directly filling the gap [[eval/gaze]] identified — existing robot datasets rarely pair gaze with the multi-camera setup modern VLA policies use.

**core**

- [GazeAct: A Human-Gaze-Annotated Manipulation Dataset for Attention-Regularized Policies](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/883df5d1af7f073fc061c9ac9980cdea36d14cdf) — Pani et al. · preprint Oct 2026
  Pairs real human gaze with multi-camera robot manipulation observations and language instructions; trains attention-regularized VLA policies that use gaze to ground instructions in correct objects and contact points. Fills the exact data gap [[eval/gaze]] identified — no existing manipulation dataset pairs gaze with the multi-camera setup modern VLA policies require. Check whether GazeAct's gaze distribution overlaps with SURF's line-drawing selection on shared manipulation scenes; if high overlap, structural priors approximate task-relevant human attention.

- [Watch Your Step: Anticipated Perception for Humanoid Locomotion](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/158b0ba4f1d36973164a56f330006880c29402a5) — Heyrman et al. · preprint Oct 2026
  Pre-selects terrain patches around predicted footsteps before the locomotion policy encoder; trains an "anticipator" module separately that outputs a sparse terrain mask, then the policy encoder sees only selected patches. Direct architectural parallel to SURF's prior module — this paper demonstrates the "pre-select before VLA backbone" pattern in a deployed humanoid system. The selector here is temporal (footstep prediction) rather than structural (line drawings), but the design choices (separate selector training, sparse input to downstream policy, patch-level granularity) are directly transferable. Check the anticipator's training objective — task-conditioned spatial selection loss or reconstruction loss?

- [Vision Token Pruning via Query-Vision Interaction Decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33215v1) — Athi et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Decomposes the query-vision interaction matrix to score visual tokens by their response to the text query; prunes low-response tokens rather than using attention-score heuristics. Directly implements and tests the mechanism [[claims/language helps]] proposes — tokens are scored by how much the language instruction needs them. Key check: does the decomposition approach outperform attention-based pruning on novel-object manipulation tasks? If the query-response score is more robust than attended-region score under distribution shift, this provides empirical support for language-conditioned selection as a generalization mechanism.

**adjacent**

- [Decoding Geometry, Not Tokens: Efficient VLMs for Robot Affordance and Trajectory Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39416v1) — Ayanthi et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Argues spatial affordance/trajectory prediction should use geometry decoders (compact off-the-shelf 3D heads) rather than autoregressive token decoding; tokenizing coordinates into text ignores spatial structure and truncates continuous precision. Supports SURF's structural-prior framing from the output side — if the correct output representation for manipulation is geometry-native, the correct input representation should also be geometry-native (structural patches, not appearance tokens). A VLM with geometry-native inputs (SURF's structural prior) and geometry-native outputs (this paper's decoder) would be consistent end-to-end.

- [DynaSlots: Slot-Centric 3D Dynamics Pretraining for Robot Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a712e2644ab5ab9742c69e404f8e2d8302e5973a) — Li et al. · preprint Oct 2026
  Slot-based pretraining that captures localized 3D dynamics (the cup moves, the scene stays); learns object-centric representations by decomposing scene-level dynamics into per-slot changes. Competing approach to SURF's structural prior for identifying task-relevant spatial regions. If DynaSlots' slot boundaries align with SURF's line-drawing selections on manipulation data, SURF's prior may be approximating object-centric segmentation — requiring justification for line drawings over direct slot-based selection. If they diverge, the mechanisms are complementary (structure captures geometric affordance; slots capture dynamic relevance).

**horizon**

- [The development of coarse and fine feature tuning in infant visual cortex](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52f04239aa1431153e6e5bcfa2e64a32faaefe24) — Dineen et al. · preprint Oct 2026
  Infant fMRI + DNN behavioral probing; finds visual cortex develops coarse-global-shape tuning before fine-local-detail tuning across the hierarchy, with different trajectories per cortical region. Grounds SURF's [[claims/iterative selection]] coarse-to-fine framing in developmental neuroscience — if the biological visual hierarchy itself develops in a coarse-first, structural-first order, this provides evolutionary/developmental motivation for a prior module that starts from global structure (line drawings) and refines toward detail. Distinct from @jiangComputationalConstraintsUnderlying (V4 topology in adults); this is about the developmental trajectory.
