**core**

- [MAST-VLA: Skill-Conditioned Adaptation with Trajectory-Affordance Priors for Multi-Skill Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32312) — Yang et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Skill-conditioned LoRA-MoE in the LLM backbone + affordance heatmap → patch-level trajectory-affordance spatial prior for the action head. This is SURF's prior module concept instantiated with affordance heatmaps rather than line drawings. The design decision — a separate spatial prior injected at the action head, derived from predicted contact/trajectory regions — is a direct competitor to [[claims/structure helps]]. If affordance priors outperform line-drawing priors on the same tasks, SURF needs either to justify structural priors as a proxy for affordances or to evaluate the two jointly.

- [PerturBot: Breaking Shortcut Priors in Vision-Language-Action Models with Perturbative Training](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26de490d3dc0ebb6c0ed53c3d53ea44a3ce72fdd) — Liu et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Introduces GroundingFscore — a metric for whether VLA decisions are grounded in task-relevant visual evidence rather than spurious correlations — plus a perturbative training framework to suppress shortcut priors. SURF's persistent shortcut risk (Sun et al.) is now a quantifiable failure mode with an existing evaluation tool. GroundingFscore could be applied to SURF's line-drawing prior to test whether structural selection actually grounds the policy in task-relevant regions or introduces new shortcuts (e.g., geometric shortcuts that co-vary with task identity in training data).

- [Beyond Attention or Similarity: Maximizing Conditional Diversity for Token Pruning in MLLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10967) — Zhang et al. · arXiv Jun 2025 · 120 citations
  Reformulates visual token pruning as maximizing conditional diversity (DPP) given the text instruction, rather than maximizing attention scores or visual similarity. With 120 citations this is now an established alternative to attention-based token selection. Directly instantiates [[claims/language helps]] via a diversity-maximization principle — but the mechanism differs from SURF's contrastive scoring. Critical comparison: does DPP-diversity selection outperform contrastive relevance-maximization on manipulation tasks where the instruction identifies a specific object/region? If diversity over relevant tokens is better than pure relevance, SURF's contrastive prior needs a diversity term.

**adjacent**

- [When Listening Becomes Easier: Scrubbing Visual Cues for Shortcut-Free VLAs](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/650d08f97f97623f1950b175e4621cca8d6e38b6) — Gerigk et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Finds that different VLM backbones vary substantially in shortcut susceptibility; introduces task scrubbing (domain-adversarial training on task identity) to suppress visual shortcuts. Paired with PerturBot, this suggests shortcut learning in VLAs is now a well-studied failure mode with reproducible diagnostics. The backbone-level susceptibility finding is important for SURF: if the VLM backbone SURF targets is high-susceptibility, the structural prior needs to actively remove shortcut visual regions rather than merely select task-relevant ones.

- [Probing Spatial State Representations in Visual Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40636v1) — Yin et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Probes whether visual foundation models represent persistent spatial state under self-motion — required for guiding action beyond single-frame recognition. Studies this through structured tasks requiring spatial memory. If the pretrained VLM backbone SURF relies on lacks spatial-state representations, patch-level structural priors are insufficient to restore them; this paper provides the diagnostic. Relevant to whether SURF's token selection is working with or against the backbone's spatial representation capacity.

**horizon**

- [End-to-end topographic networks as models of cortical map formation and human visual behaviour](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b8bf7404003befc7d91df4fd193b812a8083cbc) — Lu et al. · bioRxiv/preprint Jun 2025 · 28 citations
  Learns end-to-end topographic organization in DNNs — overcoming CNNs' hard-wired spatial invariance — and shows smooth topographic maps emerge with a metabolic (energy) benefit. The topographic organization that emerges resembles V4 functional maps and predicts human behavioral signatures. Grounding note for [[claims/v4 relevance]]: if topographic organization in DNNs is metabolically optimal and predicts behavior, V4-derived priors are not just biologically-motivated decoration but reflect an efficiency principle — select tokens that activate the spatial modules, not just the feature modules.

- [Read, Allocate, Route: Role-Coupled Tokenization for Pixel-Space Generative Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40647v1) — Yang et al. · arXiv Oct 2026
  Distinguishes token roles by frequency content: low-frequency structure needs global attention, high-frequency detail can be modeled locally. Introduces role-coupled tokenization to exploit this. Theoretical framing for SURF's whole approach: the reason structural tokens (line drawings = low-frequency edges) matter more for task grounding is that they carry global spatial structure requiring global attention, while appearance tokens are local. If the role-frequency decomposition holds for VLA, structural selection selects the tokens that actually need global transformer attention — everything else is computable locally and cheaply.

## questions

- Attention from Action: What is the novel-object failure mode of action-supervision-derived ROIs? At what distribution-shift threshold does label-free ROI emergence degrade, and does SURF's geometric prior cover that regime?
- FastVLA: Does temporal adaptation of visual sparsity require per-step recomputation of the token selector, or can SURF's geometric prior serve as a stable per-step base with iterative refinement on top?
- VLA-ACL: Does action-consistency supervision select structurally coherent regions, or semantically scattered ones? Does adding SURF's geometric prior to action-consistency supervision yield orthogonal coverage?
- CoRP: Does VIB failure on LIBERO-Goal mean SURF's contrastive alignment must produce hard selections?
- Shi et al. (Stage-Aware): Does the per-stage pruning efficacy curve show a distinct inflection after language-fusion, or is degradation gradual? Does the pattern differ for novel-object vs. in-distribution scenes?
- RAIN (Lee et al.): Does region-aware gating select geometrically coherent regions (compatible with line-drawing prior) or semantically diffuse ones (suggesting independent mechanisms)?
- Sun et al. (Artificial Foveated): Does SURF's line-drawing prior select edges that co-vary with task identity in the training distribution?
- Zhang et al. (IronMan): Does the IB-constrained representation select structurally coherent visual regions, or semantically diffuse ones?
- Magnino et al. (Metareasoning): What is the correct metareasoning objective for SURF's refinement step — minimize expected action error per compute unit, or minimize value-of-information per unit cost?
- TRIAGE (Kwon et al.): Does TRIAGE's reuse policy select geometrically consistent tokens across timesteps? Can SURF's line-drawing prior serve as the structural basis for TRIAGE's reuse component?
- ThinkProprio (Wang et al.): Does proprioception-grounded selection and structure-grounded selection select overlapping or orthogonal token sets on manipulation tasks?
- Cheng et al. (Interaction-Aligned): Does the interaction-aligned contact-region signal overlap with SURF's line-drawing selection on standard manipulation datasets?
- Watch Your Step (Heyrman et al.): What is the anticipator's training objective — task-conditioned spatial selection loss, or reconstruction? Does the anticipator generalize to novel terrain not seen in training?
- GazeAct (Pani et al.): What is the camera setup and scene distribution in GazeAct? Does SURF's line-drawing selection overlap with GazeAct's human gaze annotations on shared manipulation scenes?
- Athi et al. (Query-Vision Decomposition): Does query-response token scoring outperform attention-based selection under distribution shift (novel-object scenes)? Is the decomposition differentiable and compatible with SURF's contrastive training objective?
