**GIAVA** (Soltani et al.) is the most structurally important paper from today's digest. It uses human gaze-guided foveated ViT for robot manipulation: eye-tracking data from expert demonstrators determines which image patches the ViT receives, reducing token count while improving success on precision tasks and robustness to distractors. This is the most direct empirical instantiation of surf's selective visual processing hypothesis that the digest has surfaced — the difference is that GIAVA uses exogenous human gaze as the selection criterion rather than a structural-geometry prior. The core co-location question is whether gaze-derived foveated patches on LIBERO manipulation frames overlap with surf's line-density structural prior on the same frames. If they co-locate, surf has a strong external validation: human gaze converges on the same patches that geometric structure selects, and the prior is an approximation of expert visual attention. If they diverge, surf must either argue that structural geometry captures task-relevant information that gaze misses (e.g., contact-relevant edges that expert gaze passes over due to visual automaticity), or investigate whether the divergence is task-specific to GIAVA's task distribution vs. LIBERO's. More immediately: GIAVA's eye-tracking dataset is the most actionable evaluation resource surf has encountered. If GIAVA annotations are publicly available or reproducible on LIBERO-compatible tasks, they provide an oracle selection signal for the co-location evaluation — replacing synthetic structural priors with human-verified attention targets, which is a stronger validation than any automated metric surf could construct internally.

The V4 single-cell DL characterization paper (bioRxiv) is the second important paper today. It uses 1,200+ macaque V4 neurons and DL-based MEI synthesis to characterize single-cell tuning at scale, revealing columnar clustering in natural image feature space. This is new empirical evidence for [[claims/v4 relevance]]: if V4 feature selectivity is topographically clustered in cortical columns, the spatial organization of informative patches in a natural image follows that columnar structure. Whether this supports or constrains surf's dispersity assumption depends on the scale of clustering — tightly adjacent columns (spatially contiguous in cortex) imply that informationally-relevant patches cluster in image space, while interleaved columns imply the dispersity pattern surf's prior assumes.
