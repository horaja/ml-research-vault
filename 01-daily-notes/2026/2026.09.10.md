#### Core

**S²Prune: Spatially Structured Visual Token Pruning for Multimodal Large Language Models** — Jia, Tang & Yang (arXiv:2609.01224)
Observes that importance- and redundancy-based pruning criteria produce stable spatial biases across inputs — specifically over-selecting center and high-texture regions regardless of instruction content. Proposes spatially structured selection to break this bias. This is the existential threat to [[claims/structure helps]] flagged since day 1. If importance ≡ spatial bias (center, edges), then SURF's line-drawing prior is a reparametrization of an existing bias artifact, not a novel signal. Must read in full before positioning note. Key question: does their spatial bias finding hold under instruction-conditioned settings, or only in instruction-free scoring?

**Look, Focus, Act: Efficient and Robust Robot Learning via Human Gaze and Foveated Vision Transformers** — Chuang, Lee, Gao, Zou & Soltani (arXiv:2507.15833; 15 cites)
Uses human gaze as an attention prior combined with foveated vision transformers to reduce visual processing load in robot learning. Validates that spatial attentional priors borrowed from human behavior transfer to robot visuomotor policy. Direct competitor to SURF's line-drawing prior: gaze is a more direct behavioral signal than structural lines. SURF's advantage would need to be (a) zero-shot deployment without gaze hardware, or (b) structural prior captures task-relevant edges that gaze misses in non-human demonstrations.

**Beyond One-Size-Fits-All: Sample-Adaptive Strategy Routing for Vision Token Pruning in MLLMs** — Liang et al. (arXiv:2609.10346; 2026)
Argues that a single fixed token pruning strategy cannot generalize across diverse inputs; proposes routing to different pruning strategies based on sample characteristics. Directly constrains SURF's design: if sample-adaptive routing is required for robust performance, SURF's structural prior must either be the router or feed into one. The routing framing is complementary to SURF's selection mechanism but clarifies the specification gap: SURF does not yet have a principled fallback when structural cues are absent or ambiguous.

#### Adjacent

**Decoding Task Progress from VLA Representations** — Bhardwaj, Duan, Dan, Ma & Culbertson (arXiv:2608.13474)
Probes VLA internal representations to decode task progress and monitor models at runtime, using mechanistic interpretability methods. Relevant to SURF's efficiency/RL module: if task progress is recoverable from VLA latents, structural token selection could be conditioned on progress state without a separate progress estimator. Adds evidence that VLA representations are structured enough to support lightweight probing. Update day count: Decoding Task Progress is now in digest; see durable updates.

**StellaVLA: In-Context Structured Demonstration for Generalizable Vision-Language-Action Models** — Xu et al. (arXiv:2608.11671)
Addresses VLA OOD generalization by providing in-context structured demonstrations at inference time. Orthogonal to SURF's selection mechanism but shares the framing that structured priors at inference improve generalization. Adjacent question: does SURF's structural prior offer similar OOD benefits without additional demonstration retrieval overhead?

#### Horizon

**Top-down perceptual inference shaping the activity of early visual cortex** — Csikor, Meszéna, Ócsai & Orbán (9 cites)
Argues that top-down feedback from higher cortical areas shapes V1 activity, contrasting with purely feedforward discriminative models. Provides neuroscience grounding for the idea that task-relevant structural priors should modulate early visual representations — consistent with SURF's prior module intervening before the LLM backbone rather than after. Supporting signal for [[claims/v4 relevance]]: top-down conditioning of early visual processing is biologically well-motivated.
