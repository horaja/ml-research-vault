**VisualRouter** (Zhang et al., arXiv 2607.28463) is the most structurally important paper from today's digest. It demonstrates query-conditioned visual token sampling for long video: the natural language query provides a grounded routing signal that selects which visual tokens to pass to the model rather than sampling frames uniformly. This is a direct instantiation of [[claims/language helps]] in a different domain. The domain transfer question is non-trivial: VisualRouter routes across *temporal* frames to semantically relevant content in video (where the query is "what happens at the end"), while surf routes across *spatial* patches within a single frame to action-relevant geometry (where the instruction is "grasp the red block"). If the query-to-token routing mechanism generalizes across these two geometries, surf can cite VisualRouter as strong prior evidence for language-conditioned selection. If the mechanism is temporally specific, surf must demonstrate separately that instruction-conditioned *spatial* routing works on LIBERO frames — which is precisely what the contrastive alignment spec needs to establish. The contrastive scorer design section cannot be written without engaging this comparison.

**Beacon** (Wang et al., arXiv 2607.28595) adds pressure on [[claims/iterative selection]]. It frames agentic visual reasoning as a two-level decision: *when* to invoke expensive visual reasoning vs. proceed cheaply, and *how* to execute it when triggered. The "when" criterion is the same design question as surf's iterative selection trigger — whether the system should reallocate visual tokens at time step t+1 given the current policy state. Beacon's learned trigger outperforms rule-based activation, which is relevant if surf's trigger criterion is also rule-based (e.g., world-model mismatch threshold). The difference is task scope: Beacon operates over multi-step agent trajectories; surf operates within a single VLA forward pass or across consecutive frames of a manipulation episode.

## questions

- VisualRouter × [[claims/language helps]] domain transfer: VisualRouter routes across temporal frames in video via query grounding; surf routes across spatial patches within a frame via action instruction. Does query-to-token routing generalize from temporal (video) to spatial (manipulation) geometry, or does temporal routing depend on sequential search dynamics unavailable in single-frame manipulation contexts?
- VisualRouter × contrastive scorer design: VisualRouter uses query-grounded sampling (positive routing — query selects tokens it predicts are relevant). Surf's contrastive design uses negative contrast (patches predicted *absent* by the instruction are excluded). Are these equivalent under some parameterization, or does negative contrast capture a categorically different selection criterion than positive query routing? A dual ablation on LIBERO frames (positive-only, negative-only, contrastive) would resolve this.
- Beacon × [[claims/iterative selection]] trigger: Beacon learns "when" to invoke expensive visual reasoning; surf's iterative trigger may be rule-based (prediction error, mismatch threshold). Does a learned trigger criterion outperform a rule-based one on surf's visual budget allocation problem? What training signal supervises the trigger in surf's setting (no explicit human labels for "when to reallocate")?
- Beacon × surf scope difference: Beacon operates over multi-step agent trajectories (when to reason at each step in a task); surf operates within consecutive frames of a manipulation episode (when to reallocate tokens frame-to-frame). Are these the same trigger problem at different timescales, or does the episodic vs. frame-level timescale require qualitatively different trigger criteria?
- Training-Free VLA Acceleration × [[claims/efficiency without collapse]] composition: Action caching targets temporal redundancy (stable-scene reuse); surf targets spatial redundancy (within-frame patch selection). For a 3-7B VLA on LIBERO, what is the relative contribution of temporal (cross-frame) vs. spatial (within-frame) redundancy to total inference cost? If temporal caching provides the dominant efficiency gain, surf's spatial selection may be a second-order contribution.
- AIM × [[claims/structure helps]] co-location: AIM's spatial value maps encode intent-conditioned scene importance. Do AIM's value-map high-activation regions co-locate with surf's line-density structural prior on LIBERO contact frames? If yes, the two methods converge on the same signal (and surf's geometric prior approximates a learned value map); if no, they capture different aspects of action-relevant geometry.
- Lu et al. × [[claims/v4 relevance]] dispersity: In Lu et al.'s topographic network, does the emergent spatial organization of V4-like features exhibit the same dispersity property surf's prior assumes? The V4 dispersity prior claims that multiple non-adjacent spatial regions encode useful structural geometry; Lu et al.'s emergent topography may or may not reproduce this inter-region independence.
- R-D survey × surf budget policy tractability: Wu et al.'s survey covers rate-distortion-type optimization paradigms. Which class of methods is tractable for surf's visual budget policy at VLA inference speed (3-7B, 30-50 Hz target)? The survey should identify whether a learn-to-allocate-compute formulation is feasible within the constraint or whether a simpler heuristic budget policy is the practical choice.
