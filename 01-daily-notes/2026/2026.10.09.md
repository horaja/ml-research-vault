# 2026-10-09

## opener

Positioning note is day 45. Two papers land today that corroborate SURF's structural claim from new angles: SpatialHarness diagnoses spatial observability as the failure mode in fine manipulation for frontier VLMs and proposes test-time spatial scaffolding as a fix — this is SURF's claim reframed as a test-time remedy rather than a training-time structural prior. The key question is whether both mechanisms target the same spatial gaps, and whether SURF's train-time prior subsumes what SpatialHarness applies at test time.

ViMoD (Look Back, Think Ahead) provides the closest published architecture to [[claims/iterative selection]]: one-shot visual token pruning fails because visual evidence needs shift across reasoning stages; ViMoD retrieves visual tokens on demand as reasoning unfolds. This is a stronger instantiation of iterative selection than Have I Seen Enough (10/07), which only showed a readiness signal exists — ViMoD shows an architecture that acts on it. Read before designing the iterative selection module.

Spatial Language Modeling (Wu et al.) directly tests the structure helps + language helps claims together: scene contours, language goals, action targets, and future states are encoded in a shared vocabulary of discrete coordinates + semantic tokens. An autoregressive transformer trained on this vocabulary handles manipulation policy jointly. This is a strong architectural validation for SURF's joint structure + language approach — but also a competitor: if a single vocabulary achieves this without a separate contrastive prior module, SURF's architectural separation must be justified.

Cao et al. is day 10. Unread. Read today.

**core**

- [SpatialHarness: Test-Time Spatial Scaffolding for Fine Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12457v1) — Wang et al. · arXiv
  Identifies insufficient spatial observability (not policy capability) as the failure mode in fine manipulation for frontier VLMs (GPT-6 Astra). Proposes test-time spatial scaffolding to expose task-critical spatial relationships. Directly confirms SURF's diagnosis — structural spatial information is the missing ingredient — while framing the fix as test-time intervention rather than training-time structural prior. Key question: does test-time scaffolding and SURF's train-time prior target the same spatial gaps, or does each fix failures the other misses?

- [Look Back, Think Ahead: Visual Memory on Demand for Efficient Multimodal Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12060v1) — Xue et al. · arXiv
  ViMoD: lightweight framework that stores visual tokens in a memory bank and retrieves them on demand as reasoning stage requirements shift. One-shot pruning fails because visual evidence needs evolve across stages — fixed compression cannot anticipate which tokens are needed later. Strongest published architecture for [[claims/iterative selection]]: establishes both the motivation (evidence needs shift) and a mechanism (on-demand retrieval). Check whether ViMoD's retrieval key is semantic or spatial — if spatial, SURF's structural prior is a natural retrieval key.

- [Unifying Policy Learning and State Prediction through Spatial Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12172v1) — Wu et al. · arXiv
  Represents scene contours, goals, action targets, and future states with a shared vocabulary of discrete coordinates and semantic tokens; a task-specific grammar organizes these into spatial sequences for an autoregressive transformer. Simultaneously tests [[claims/structure helps]] (contours as the spatial signal) and [[claims/language helps]] (language goals in shared vocabulary). If this unified vocabulary approach performs well without a separate contrastive prior module, SURF must articulate what architectural separation buys beyond end-to-end unified training.

**adjacent**

- [ContourVLA: A Closed-Loop Perception-Action Contour Policy for Generalized Referring Expression Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12107v1) — Zhang et al. · arXiv
  Closed-loop VLA policy using contours (structural boundary signal) for language-conditioned referring expression segmentation. The contour = structural signal connection is the same signal SURF's line drawing prior extracts. Different task (segmentation vs. manipulation token selection), but check: does language-conditioned contour selection generalize from segmentation to manipulation under the same architectural template?

- [PIER: An Evidence-Gated Execution Interface for Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12123v1) — Li et al. · arXiv
  Execution-authorization interface that separates evidence checks from hardware control: evaluates declared spatial support against current observations before allowing execution, with stage-scoped re-observation for failed checks. Directly operationalizes [[claims/iterative selection]] as an execution gate — the stopping criterion for iterative visual access is a declarative evidence check, not a trained RL policy. Compare with ViMoD (memory-based retrieval) and Have I Seen Enough (linear readout) as three different implementations of iterative visual access.

**horizon**

- [One Block, Multiple Depths: Recurrent Vision Transformers with Depth-Programmed Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12448v1) — Bulat et al. · arXiv
  reViT: single Transformer block applied recurrently with depth-programmed experts (FFN as convex combination of a shared expert bank, indexed by normalized depth coordinate) matches full-depth encoder accuracy at comparable FLOPs. Depth efficiency from weight reuse + expert interpolation rather than width reduction. ML insight for SURF's iterative selection design: a recurrent single-block selector architecture applied across iterations may achieve depth-equivalent selection quality with fewer parameters.

- [SplitJEPA: Learning Invariant and Variant Latent Worlds without Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12349v1) — Hua et al. · arXiv
  Decomposes latent world representation into invariant factors (stable across camera shift / lighting change) and variant factors (changing with task state) without reconstruction. Relevant to SURF's stop-gradient architecture (Wiring Matters): the structural geometric features SURF's prior extracts are invariant-like (stable across robot state changes), while the task-relevant VLA backbone activations are variant. SplitJEPA's decomposition could formalize the boundary where stop-gradient belongs.
