Both are the last named holes in the structural-prior axis of the positioning note. The note can be written today without any remaining gap markers.

The anchor paper today is GeoScaffold (Beyond Token Importance): a simple geometric baseline — uniform stride sampling — outperforms semantic pruning in VLA tasks at certain compression ratios. The mechanism is the spatial coverage radius: semantic-only selection clusters high-attention tokens, creating spatial blind spots. GeoScaffold fixes this with region-partitioned farthest-point sampling and achieves 93.2% success at 20% token budget. This is the empirical grounding the structural-prior axis of the positioning note was missing. It also reframes the [[claims/structure helps]] claim from "line drawings improve classification" to "geometric coverage quality is the right objective for VLA token selection."

GSPA's geo = local correspondence features + object detection, fused via a semantic-priority gate, injected into selected OpenVLA attention layers. Not depth maps, not line drawings. SURF uses a different geometric modality — which means GSPA is a direct competitor baseline, not a prior that SURF extends. Write both into the positioning note's baseline table.

- [Beyond Token Importance: Preserving Spatial Scaffolds for Efficient VLA Inference](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef62d971f2796de5224660cade47c195f294f5f3) — Chen et al. · preprint · 2026-09-29
  A Stride baseline (uniform geometric sampling) outperforms semantic pruning at moderate compression in VLA tasks; collapses under extreme compression due to spatial blind spots quantified by a spatial coverage radius metric. GeoScaffold addresses this: region-partitioned budget allocation + farthest-point intra-region sampling; 93.2% success at 20% tokens on pi0.5 and LIBERO. This is the clearest published empirical argument for [[claims/structure helps]] in a VLA context: semantic-only selection fails by creating coverage gaps, and the fix is geometric. SURF's baseline comparison table needs GeoScaffold as a training-free structural competitor.

**adjacent**

- [VPRune: Efficient Training-free Pre-LLM Visual Token Pruning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/abcacc5f990610ea511c80381d696fae0c0830b2) — Lv et al. · preprint · 2026-09-21
  Names three failure modes of aggressive VLM token pruning: text-guided selection bias (language-only pruning discards visually important tokens), information loss, and positional distortion from sequence compaction. The "text-guided selection bias" term is the one SURF needs: if instruction conditioning systematically selects semantically obvious tokens and misses contact-rich structural patches, [[claims/language helps]] has an adversarial regime. SURF's structural prior is positioned to correct this bias — VPRune makes the failure mode explicit and names it, giving the positioning note a direct vocabulary.

- [SAVLA: Symmetry-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models for Robotic Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d1f93e6c5590ad9a52d8908f356fd1f264dcb0f5) — Li et al. · preprint · 2026-09-15 · 1 cite
  VLAs fail outside demonstrated pose distributions because geometric competence is acquired only from demonstrations, not built in. SAVLA adds equivariance to the action head. Composability question for SURF: SURF applies geometric structure at the input (horizontal token selection), SAVLA applies it at the output (action equivariance). These operate at different pipeline stages — check whether input-stage structural selection and output-stage action equivariance are orthogonal or interfering, specifically whether SURF's selected token set preserves the spatial geometry SAVLA needs.

**horizon**

- [Compact deep neural network models of the visual cortex](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18d00056b2cd000d3b365ac5c17e11c885bf0b2f) — Cowley, Stan, Pillow, Smith · Nature 2026 · 7 cites

- [Top-down perceptual inference shaping the activity of early visual cortex](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d34bc84826ed09fe3579539e126bfd8ef26ea8d) — Csikor, Meszéna, Ócsai, Orbán · bioRxiv 2025 · 9 cites

Grounds [[claims/iterative selection]]: if early visual cortex is shaped by top-down generative inference, iterative refinement in SURF has a computational analog in biological vision. Caution: V1/V2 circuits ≠ V4 — the generative hierarchy here may not extend to the shape/texture domain where SURF's prior operates.
