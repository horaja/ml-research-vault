Tuesday, August 4. Contrastive alignment spec unwritten — 29 weeks. GSPA unread — day 52. "When Attention Collapses" STS unread — day 41. Visual Access Boundaries unread — day 21.

**AtVLA** (Cui et al., arXiv 2608.02197) is the most structurally important paper from today's digest. It diagnoses attention artifacts in VLA vision encoders — specifically that limited global-token capacity causes task-relevant spatial information (object location, depth ordering, local geometry) to spill into low-information patch tokens, corrupting attention distributions. AtVLA fixes this with learnable register tokens inserted into the visual encoder, trained end-to-end on embodied data alone. The result: 94.2% → 98.4% on LIBERO, and 46.5% → 69.0% on a real-world single-view benchmark. The further finding — that attention rectification alone is insufficient, and that uncertain predictions require uncertainty-gated local refinement (crop, re-encode, append to prefix) — is a direct instantiation of surf's [[claims/iterative selection]] hypothesis, applied at the encoder rather than the selection-budget level. Two questions are now critical: (1) **differentiation**: AtVLA achieves 98.4% on LIBERO through attention cleanup + register tokens + uncertainty-gated refinement, while surf proposes structural geometry priors + language conditioning + iterative budget allocation. If AtVLA's mechanism is sufficient, surf must claim either a different operational regime (training-free, lower compute, better sample efficiency, cross-task generalization) or demonstrate that structural priors and language conditioning add a further improvement on top of AtVLA's baseline. (2) **convergence**: AtVLA's uncertainty-gated local refinement triggers on ~30% of replanning steps and produces 1.4–1.6× compute overhead. Surf's iterative selection claims to be cheaper. The comparison between AtVLA's crop-and-re-encode and surf's budget-constrained patch selection is the first concrete compute benchmark surf needs to beat. AtVLA is now surf's primary adjacent reference — read before writing the contrastive alignment spec.

- [Messages, Not Tokens: Grounded Coresets for Faithful VLM Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02134) — Qian et al. · arXiv 2026-08-04
  Coreset-based visual token compression that treats token importance as a collective, signed attention message from the visual population to the language decoder rather than per-token independent scores — resolves the Top-K pathology where equally-scored tokens carry redundant information. Competitor for surf's token selection: if coreset compression achieves faithfulness without structural geometry or language conditioning, the information-theoretic framing may subsume surf's positional prior. The signed-message criterion is different from surf's structural score (geometry vs. message coherence), but the practical question is whether the two converge on the same patches on LIBERO manipulation frames.

**adjacent**

- [Grounded Semantic Re-Binding for Robust Instruction Generalization in Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02497) — Yin & Zhang · arXiv 2026-08-04
  VLAs fail catastrophically on paraphrased instructions despite retaining correct task identity internally; root cause is architectural coupling of instruction semantics with positional-token routing. Re-binding modules decouple semantic identity from token routing paths, restoring generalization without data scaling. Relevant to [[claims/language helps]]: if instruction-routing coupling is a systematic failure mode in existing VLAs, surf's contrastive scorer — which explicitly produces a language-conditioned spatial attention map decoupled from the VLA backbone's routing — may be inherently more robust to instruction variation than the base architecture. The decoupling question also applies to surf's scorer: does the contrastive objective decouple language instruction from spatial token coordinates, or does it re-introduce positional coupling?

- [WCM: A World Critic Model for Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6baeeb619be6041013d59387d1099f5653ec7ed9) — Fei et al. · 2026
  World Critic Model for VLA RL post-training that handles partial observability by maintaining a compressed world-state latent across observation history, rather than single-frame critic inputs. Resolves the mismatch between VLA's partially observable control loop and standard RL critics. Adjacent to [[claims/iterative selection]]: WCM's world-state latent is the learned compressed representation of "what has been observed and whether the world state has changed" — structurally similar to surf's iterative selection stop criterion (when does the current token selection provide sufficient information for the next action?). The difference: WCM operates at the semantic task-state level; surf at the visual patch level. If WCM's world-state latent predicts when re-observation is needed, it's a trajectory-level analog of surf's frame-level reallocation trigger.

- [ChainVLA: Chaining Vision-Language-Action Queries through a Unified Execution State for Long-Horizon Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02326) — Huang et al. · arXiv 2026-08-04
  ChainVLA maintains a unified execution state across action-chunked VLA queries — what earlier actions established remains visible to later planning steps — without recomputing from scratch at each re-plan step. Adjacent to [[claims/iterative selection]]: ChainVLA's execution state is the trajectory-level analog of surf's per-frame iterative selection policy. If the critical information in a manipulation episode can be distilled into a compact execution state, surf's iterative visual patch reallocation can be bounded to only the frames where the execution state diverges from prediction — the execution-state divergence is surf's trigger criterion operationalized at the trajectory level.

**horizon**

- [HAFI-VLM: A Frequency Perspective for Diagnosing and Enhancing Visual Perception in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02124) — Cui et al. · arXiv 2026-08-04
  Finds that pretrained vision encoders exhibit spectral response rigidity: layerwise spectral profiles change only marginally under downstream fine-tuning, meaning encoders cannot adapt their frequency extraction to task-specific evidence requirements. Patch tokens reaching the language decoder are systematically biased toward low-frequency, global content. Diagnostic threat to [[claims/structure helps]]: surf's structural geometry prior selects line-rich patches containing high-spatial-frequency structure (edges, contours). If the vision encoder suppresses high-frequency content at output, surf's selected patches may carry the correct spatial information at the pixel level but present it to the LLM in a low-frequency–dominated token representation. The severity of this threat depends on where the frequency suppression occurs: if it's pre-selection (encoder early layers), structural selection is undermined; if it's post-selection (decoder projection), the prior still narrows attention but the token information is lost. HAFI-VLM's diagnostic methodology should be applied to surf's encoder before the spec is written.

## questions

- AtVLA × [[claims/iterative selection]] trigger equivalence: AtVLA's uncertainty-gated local refinement triggers on ~30% of replanning steps using action disagreement among sampled chunks as the uncertainty signal. Is surf's iterative token reallocation criterion (world-model mismatch, prediction confidence, attention entropy) equivalent to AtVLA's action disagreement criterion, or does it capture a different failure mode? If action chunk disagreement is the dominant surf-relevant failure signal, surf may not need a separate visual token selection trigger — AtVLA's criterion could be reused.
- AtVLA × surf differentiation: AtVLA achieves 98.4% LIBERO at 1.4–1.6× base compute. Surf must claim either: (a) a different target regime (training-free, lower compute, cross-task generalization), (b) a higher success rate, or (c) better sample efficiency. Which of these is the primary differentiation claim surf should pursue? The spec cannot be written without answering this.
- AtVLA × [[claims/structure helps]] relationship: AtVLA uses register tokens to clean up attention; surf proposes structural priors to select the right patches before encoding. Are these approaches complementary (apply surf's structural selection to reduce token count before AtVLA's register tokens clean up residual artifacts) or competing (AtVLA's register tokens make structural selection unnecessary because they redirect attention anyway)? The ordering matters: if register tokens absorb the structural information task the prior was meant to supply, surf's contribution collapses to a compute optimization.
- Messages Not Tokens × [[claims/structure helps]] co-location: The coreset criterion selects tokens whose collective signed attention message to the language decoder is maximally faithful. Do coreset-selected tokens on LIBERO manipulation frames co-locate with surf's line-density structural prior? If yes, structural geometry approximates the coreset criterion; if no, they capture different aspects of action-relevant information (geometric structure vs. message coherence).
- HAFI-VLM × [[claims/structure helps]] frequency threat: If pretrained vision encoders systematically suppress high-frequency content in output patch tokens, surf's structural geometry prior selects patches containing high-spatial-frequency edges but the encoder outputs low-frequency representations. Does surf's prior still help by preserving *which* low-frequency representations reach the LLM (spatial locality of geometric structure), or does frequency suppression eliminate the structural signal before it reaches the decoder?
- Grounded Semantic Re-Binding × [[claims/language helps]] decoupling: Re-Binding fixes instruction-routing coupling by adding re-binding modules. Does surf's contrastive scorer — which produces a language-conditioned patch selection map — decouple instruction semantics from positional-token routing, or does it re-introduce a new form of coupling between language signal and spatial coordinate system?
- WCM × [[claims/iterative selection]] world-state latent: WCM compresses observation history into a world-state latent for the RL critic. If surf's iterative selection policy were expressed as a POMDP critic over the visual budget allocation problem, what would the analogous "world-state latent" be — compressed attention history, running structural prior estimate, task-progress signal?
