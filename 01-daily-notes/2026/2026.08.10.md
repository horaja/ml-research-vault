**Depth-Wise Probing and Pruning of the Planning Token** (Babu et al., arXiv 2608.07361) is today's most architecturally relevant paper. Using a logit-lens approach, the authors decode the VLA's planning token from each of 32 LLM decoder layers and find that action decodability saturates well before the final layer — roughly 67% of the network depth can be pruned without accuracy loss. For surf, this provides a direct measurement of where in LLM depth action commitment stabilizes: if the planning token is usable at layer ~11, then input-stage visual token reduction (surf's approach) eliminates compute overhead across every layer of the stabilization process, not just the tail. This complements Visual Access Boundaries (unread, day 27) as a second data point that the architectural window for token selection is wider than commonly assumed. The driving domain is a scope limitation; the depth-stabilization finding should be verified for manipulation VLAs before treating it as conclusive.

**TEMPO** (Liu & Yang, arXiv 2608.07314) provides the most concrete training-order constraint for surf's contrastive scorer to date. Applying uniform RL gradient to all VLA components degrades the pretrained visual representation — the model "adapts" by disrupting its own visual understanding. TEMPO's solution is a two-timescale framework: freeze the vision-language backbone, train only a decoupled action head with RL. The direct implication for surf: the contrastive scorer must be trained as a separate pre-training stage on frozen VLM representations, before action fine-tuning begins. Co-training scorer and action head allows the RL gradient to erode the visual representation the scorer is scoring — a failure mode the spec's training section must explicitly address.

**adjacent**

- [Cross-View Action Consistency for Camera-Robust Vision-Language-Action Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06965) — Huang et al. · arXiv 2026-08-10
  VLA fine-tuned on a fixed scene camera fails under modest viewpoint shift even when task, objects, language, and robot state are unchanged; cross-view consistency training recovers robustness. Relevant to [[claims/structure helps]]: line-geometry features are more viewpoint-invariant than RGB/texture — if viewpoint robustness is a real VLA failure mode (this paper shows it is), surf's structural prior may provide robustness as a second contribution axis beyond efficiency; the spec evaluation section should include a viewpoint-shift test on LIBERO.

- [RoRA: Role-Oriented Regional Allocation for Visual Token Pruning in MLLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07088) — Lu et al. · arXiv 2026-08-10

Direct competing method for surf's token selection: RoRA allocates by semantic region coverage (detection-based), surf by structural geometry (line density + language). Add to spec baseline suite — the key comparison is whether geometric structure captures affordance boundaries and contact surfaces that pure region coverage allocation misses.

## questions

- Depth-Wise Probing × surf architecture: If action commitment stabilizes at layer ~11 in a 32-layer driving VLA, what is the equivalent stabilization depth in manipulation VLAs (OpenVLA 7B, pi0)? The domain matters — driving has a single planning token; manipulation VLAs use action-chunk heads. Is the logit-lens approach applicable to manipulation VLA's action head tokens, or does the multi-token action representation require a different probe?
- Depth-Wise Probing × input-stage vs. mid-network selection: Babu et al. prune depth after token selection; surf prunes tokens before depth. Are these dual controls — depth pruning and token pruning — composable, and if so, what is the correct order? Token-prune → full depth, or full tokens → depth-prune, or both simultaneously?
- TEMPO × [[experiments/contrastive alignment]] training order: TEMPO freezes the VLM backbone during RL post-training. Should surf's contrastive scorer be trained in a completely separate stage (frozen VLM → scorer pre-training → action head RL), or can the scorer be trained jointly with a frozen backbone if the action RL is isolated to a separate head? What is the correct gradient isolation design?
- Cross-View × [[claims/structure helps]] robustness test: Does surf's line-density prior select the same geometric tokens across modest viewpoint shifts on LIBERO — and if so, is this testable without additional multi-camera data? One approach: apply homographic viewpoint perturbation to existing LIBERO frames and measure whether line-density-selected tokens remain consistent across perturbations.
- RoRA × spec baseline: RoRA uses off-the-shelf object detection for region allocation. On LIBERO manipulation frames, where the "relevant object" is specified by the language instruction, does RoRA's detection-based region coverage overlap with surf's language-conditioned structural selection? If they co-locate on the target object but diverge on affordance boundaries and contact surfaces, surf's differential claim is grounded.
- DINA × [[claims/v4 relevance]] operationalization: DINA's dual-tower V1 alignment works with existing neural population data. Is @jiangComputationalConstraintsUnderlying's digital twin (V4 responses from calcium imaging via @wangLargescaleCalciumImaging2024) sufficient in resolution and coverage to run DINA-style dual-tower alignment for V4? What resolution of latent alignment is needed to produce a per-patch V4 relevance score usable as a selection prior on LIBERO frames?
