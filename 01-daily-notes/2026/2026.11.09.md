**core**

- [Seeing What Matters: Benchmarking Task-Conditioned Actionable Spatial Belief for Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09794) — Jian & Xu · arXiv Nov 2026
  Introduces ACTSB (actionable spatial belief): the capacity to acquire the particular scene evidence that makes the current manipulation command executable. A view informative for one task can be useless for another at the same workstation. Benchmark across three tabletop domains (coffee, drawer/cabinet, microwave/appliance); quantifies how well a model can identify task-relevant spatial regions under instruction conditioning. For SURF: this is the most direct formalization of SURF's core problem (what to look at, conditioned on the command) published to date. Evaluation design and vocabulary for SURF's positioning note and eval plan. Read before writing the eval spec.

- [Attend at the Right Resolution: Head-Adaptive Sparse Attention via Query-Key Interaction Structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09700) — Raza et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Sparse attention in LLMs: identifies per-head selection granularity and sparsity patterns from the intrinsic structure of pre-trained query-key interactions rather than runtime heuristics. Finding: different heads exhibit different optimal granularities; fixed granularity across heads underperforms structure-derived assignment. For SURF: direct parallel at the LLM-attention level to SURF's design choice between a fixed structural prior (line drawings) and a data-driven interaction-structure-based selector. If interaction structure determines optimal granularity in LLMs, the same logic suggests SURF's contrastive alignment objective (learning from instruction-patch interaction) may be necessary, not optional.

**adjacent**

- [HiMEM: Hippocampus-Inspired Memory Consolidation, Pruning, and Composition for VLA Models in 3D Scenes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09355) — Zhao et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Introduces hippocampal memory consolidation for VLAs: synthesizes episodic memories into generalizable manipulation principles (selective episodic memory compression), pruning redundant scene layout details while preserving task-relevant interaction patterns. Applied to long-horizon VLA tasks in 3D scenes. For SURF: competes with structural spatial priors as a strategy for reducing visual redundancy — but at the temporal/episodic scale rather than within-frame spatial scale. The pruning criterion (what details generalize across tasks) is closely related to SURF's question of what structural features are task-invariant. Read to understand how the field is approaching "what to remember" vs. SURF's "what to look at."

- [On Agentic Manipulation Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09892) — Chen et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Argues that generalist AI models (GPT-6, etc.) control manipulators more reliably than specialist VLAs and world-action models on a range of tasks. Systematically analyzes agentic manipulation policies (AMPs) where a generalist model commands the manipulator directly. For SURF: the most direct challenge to the VLA-centric framing yet. If generalist AMPs outperform VLAs on manipulation, SURF's efficiency improvements on VLA inference need to generalize to or complement the AMP framework — or SURF must argue the AMP approach fails in the latency/closed-loop regime where visual selection matters most.

**horizon**

- [Graded Convolutional Networks: Scale Invariance without the Scale and Robustness to Unknown Blur](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09878) — Shaska · arXiv Nov 2026
  Formalizes scale invariance via the graded jet space of an image: a dilation by λ multiplies order-k derivatives by λ^{-k}, so a local feature is dilation-invariant iff it factors through the scale-normalized jet. Under unknown blur, the retraction changes only by a scale factor, while the scale-normalized jet acquires an irremovable grading factor. For SURF: provides a mathematical framework where edge/line features (first-order jet components) are the natural, scale-invariant building block. This is principled grounding for why a line-drawing prior generalizes across imaging conditions and camera distances — without biological overclaim. The gradient / edge is structurally the most stable feature under dilation and blur.
