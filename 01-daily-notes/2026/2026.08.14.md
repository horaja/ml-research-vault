Friday, August 14. Contrastive alignment spec unwritten — 30 weeks. GSPA unread — day 63. "When Attention Collapses" STS unread — day 51. Visual Access Boundaries unread — day 31. AtVLA unread — day 13.

Today's most important paper is **"Attention from Action, for Action"** (Zhuang et al., arXiv 2608.13422). It shows that visual ROI-selection bottlenecks that *separate where to look from how to act* consistently improve visuomotor policy learning — and that these bottlenecks can emerge from action prediction alone, without a structural prior. This is the clearest direct test of surf's thesis to date. It either validates the ROI-selection approach (surf's core claim) or threatens the structural-prior novelty claim (if a learned emergent bottleneck matches line-density selection on manipulation frames, the prior is redundant). This paper must be read before writing the spec differentiation section. It supersedes AtVLA as the most urgent read.

A second paper: **"End-to-end topographic networks as models of cortical map formation"** (Lu et al., S2 4b8bf740) shows that topographic cortical organization and human perceptual effects emerge jointly from a single end-to-end learning objective. This closes the biological narrative loop: Piper et al. (subcortical front-end), Cortinovis et al. (action topography in V4/IT), Finzi et al. (stream specialization from topographic objective), and now Lu et al. (end-to-end emergence of cortical maps). Together these four papers provide a coherent multi-level biological grounding for [[claims/v4 relevance]].

**core**

- [Attention from Action, for Action: Emergent Visual Bottlenecks for Policy Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13422) — Zhuang, Wang et al. · arXiv 2026-08-14
  Demonstrates that visual bottlenecks focusing policy inputs on task-relevant ROIs improve data-efficient visuomotor learning; the key design is separating *where to look* from *how to act*, and the authors show these bottlenecks can emerge directly from action prediction without explicit structural supervision. Direct test of surf's core thesis: if the emergent bottleneck and line-density-selected patches co-locate on LIBERO frames, surf's structural prior is a training-free approximation to the learned optimum; if they diverge, surf's prior captures something the emergent bottleneck misses. Must read before writing [[experiments/contrastive alignment]] differentiation section — supersedes AtVLA in read urgency.

- [PosA-VLA: Enhancing Action Generation via Pose-Conditioned Anchor Attention](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d77a24298f448ff9f6aeabec20137aebeb4534e9) — Li et al. · S2 2026
  Uses 3D body/end-effector pose coordinates to define spatial anchor points that gate visual attention in a VLA; treats geometric pose information as the structural prior for attention allocation. A distinct structural geometric prior from a different signal source (pose, not line density). The key comparison: do pose-conditioned anchors and line-density-selected tokens co-locate on affordance regions in LIBERO frames? If yes, both priors approximate the same structure and pose is an easier-to-obtain signal; if no, they capture complementary geometry (pose = contact locus, line density = affordance boundary).

- [Decoding Task Progress from VLA Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13474) — Bhardwaj, Duan et al. · arXiv 2026-08-14
  Develops probing tools to decode what task-progress information is encoded in VLA representations at each step — essentially a logit-lens approach applied to task state rather than action. Direct evaluation method for surf: if surf's structural token selection drops the bottom-k patches by line density, does the VLA's task-progress encoding degrade proportionally or remain stable? A stable encoding under token reduction would directly support [[claims/efficiency without collapse]]; degradation would constrain the minimum visual budget.

**adjacent**

- [RayViT: Ray-Conditioned Visual Representations for Viewpoint-Robust Imitation Learning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8009e0138c252bd117f45b37e49d31eff0314467) — Wang et al. · S2
  Conditions robot policy visual representations on camera ray directions (geometric camera model) to produce viewpoint-invariant features; addresses VLA viewpoint fragility (confirmed by Cross-View Action Consistency, surfaced 8/10) via camera geometry rather than image-space structure. Structural approach to robustness from a different geometric signal than surf's line density: rays capture the camera coordinate frame; line density captures image-plane edge structure. Relevant to whether surf's robustness axis (viewpoint-invariant structural prior) can be distinguished from raw camera-geometry conditioning.

- [Temporal GRPO: Beyond Trajectory-Level Credit in Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13026) — Zhou, Gao et al. · arXiv 2026-08-14
  Introduces step-level GRPO credit assignment for VLA RL post-training, replacing trajectory-level reward attribution with per-step advantage estimates. Relevant to surf's training design: TEMPO (8/10) constrains scorer pre-training before action RL; WCM (8/12) requires multi-frame critics; Temporal GRPO now introduces step-level credit — the right level of granularity for surf's per-step patch selection to interact with RL reward attribution. Determines whether surf's contrastive scorer can be trained jointly with step-level GRPO or requires a separate pre-training stage.

- [AffordSim: A Scalable Data Generator and Benchmark for Affordance-Aware Robotic Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1adbe7f56d7a64a14e86c54f8156b0357ee8a4a9) — Li et al. · S2
  Scalable affordance data generation + benchmark for manipulation tasks with ground-truth contact regions and functional object parts. Direct evaluation candidate for surf: IoU between line-density top-k patch selection and AffordSim's ground-truth affordance masks would test whether structural geometry recovers affordance-relevant patches without task-specific training — the core assumption behind [[claims/structure helps]]. A structural prior that achieves high IoU on AffordSim would ground the claim empirically.

**horizon**

- [End-to-end topographic networks as models of cortical map formation and human visual perception](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b8bf7404003befc7d91df4fd193b812a8083cbc) — Lu, Doerig et al. · S2
  End-to-end model trained on visual tasks with a topographic spatial similarity constraint reproduces both cortical map formation and human behavioral perceptual effects (crowding, flanker effects, peripheral performance) without separate objectives for each. Closes the biological narrative loop for [[claims/v4 relevance]]: a single topographic objective is necessary and sufficient to produce the organizational structure that Cortinovis et al. and Finzi et al. describe empirically. Strongest computational grounding for surf's topographic biological argument to date.

## questions

- Attention from Action × [[claims/structure helps]] differentiation: If Zhuang et al.'s emergent visual bottleneck — trained purely on action prediction without a structural prior — selects the same ROIs as line-density selection on LIBERO manipulation frames, then surf's structural prior is a zero-shot approximation of what the emergent bottleneck learns. But if they diverge (e.g., emergent bottleneck concentrates on task-progress-critical regions while line density concentrates on structural boundaries), the divergence is the differential claim: structural priors select affordance boundaries the emergent bottleneck misses, especially in zero-shot generalization. How do we test this without running both methods on LIBERO frames?
- PosA-VLA × structural prior design space: PosA-VLA uses 3D pose coordinates (body kinematics) as the anchor signal; surf uses 2D line density (image-space structure). If affordance regions are consistently reachable from pose + instruction (without looking at image structure), then pose-based anchoring and image-space structural priors are competing approaches to the same problem. Which is stronger at zero-shot affordance localization on unseen objects? Pose depends on task-specific kinematic structure; line density depends on image-specific geometric structure. Where do they fail differently?
- Decoding Task Progress × evaluation design: Bhardwaj et al. decode task progress from VLA representations. Does their probing methodology work at the token level (per-patch progress encoding) or only at the whole-representation level? If per-patch, applying their probe to surf's selected-token subset vs. discarded-token subset would directly measure whether line-density selection preserves progress-relevant information — an empirical test of [[claims/efficiency without collapse]] at the representation level.
- RayViT × viewpoint robustness mechanism: RayViT conditions on camera rays; Cross-View Action Consistency (8/10) showed viewpoint failure in VLA; surf's line-density prior is claimed to be more viewpoint-invariant than RGB. Are ray conditioning and structural line density solving the same robustness problem from different angles, or are they addressing different failure modes? Ray conditioning handles metric viewpoint shifts (homographies); line density provides scale/texture-invariant structure. Testing whether they fail on the same scenes would clarify whether they are complementary.
- Temporal GRPO × spec training section: With TEMPO (frozen VLM, separate action head) + Temporal GRPO (step-level credit), what is the correct scorer training pipeline? Hypothesis: (1) pre-train contrastive scorer on frozen VLM embeddings; (2) freeze scorer; (3) RL-train action head with Temporal GRPO step-level credit. Does step-level GRPO apply to the action head only, or does it provide a gradient path back to the scorer? If scorer is frozen during step 3, step-level credit is irrelevant to scorer training. If scorer is co-trained during step 3, TEMPO's representation-disruption risk applies.
- AffordSim × eval pipeline: AffordSim generates ground-truth affordance regions for diverse manipulation tasks. What is the coverage of AffordSim object categories relative to LIBERO? If LIBERO and AffordSim share object categories (kitchen utensils, containers, tools), surf can evaluate structural-prior alignment with affordance ground truth using AffordSim masks without additional annotation effort. Is AffordSim simulation-only or does it provide real-world equivalent data?
