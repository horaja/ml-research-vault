Friday, July 31. Contrastive alignment spec unwritten — 25 weeks. GSPA unread — day 48. "When Attention Collapses" (STS) unread — day 37. Visual Access Boundaries unread — day 17.

**FA-RDP** (Zhuo et al.) is the most structurally informative paper from today's digest. It shows that contact-rich manipulation episodes naturally decompose into two phases: pre-contact (diverse action modes, geometric constraints loose) and post-contact (reactive, geometric constraints tight). A contact-onset detector triggers a mode switch in the diffusion policy. This decomposition maps directly onto surf's open architecture question: should the structural prior apply uniformly across an episode, or only in the pre-contact phase? If line-density selects candidate contact-geometry patches in the pre-contact phase but post-contact tokens are better identified by motion or force signal, then the budget policy must phase-condition patch selection. FA-RDP provides a concrete, implementable trigger — contact-onset detection from proprioception or visual motion — that could serve as surf's iterative selection signal.

**ReToken** (Xiao et al.) proposes a single learnable embedding that selects sparse visual tokens from a pre-filled visual KV cache given a query, without additional modules or recomputation. This is the cleanest single-embedding instantiation of surf's instruction-conditioned scorer idea that has appeared so far. The design difference from surf's current concept: ReToken selects *after* the ViT encodes all tokens — encoding cost persists, LLM attention cost is reduced. If surf's computational bottleneck is LLM attention over visual tokens (not ViT encode), ReToken's KV-cache approach is the right architecture. If the bottleneck is ViT encoding, surf must select before encode and ReToken's design does not apply.

**core**

- [FA-RDP: A Frequency-Adaptive Reactive Diffusion Policy for Contact-Rich Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28596) — Zhuo et al. · arXiv 2026-07-31
  Dual-mode diffusion policy that switches at contact onset: pre-contact phase preserves diverse action distributions (geometric constraints loose); post-contact phase collapses to reactive single-mode (geometric constraints tight). For [[claims/structure helps]]: if surf's line-density prior preferentially selects pre-contact candidate-geometry patches, and post-contact tokens are identified by motion/force signal rather than geometric structure, the structural prior is phase-restricted — a specific and important scope constraint. FA-RDP's contact-onset detector (proprioceptive or visual) is the most concrete trigger mechanism yet for surf's iterative selection signal.

- [ReToken: One Token to Improve Vision-Language Models for Visual Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28627) — Xiao et al. · arXiv 2026-07-31
  Single learnable retrieval embedding selects a sparse set of query-relevant visual tokens from a pre-filled visual KV cache. For [[claims/language helps]]: this is the minimal-parameter version of surf's instruction-conditioned scorer — one embedding per query, no auxiliary scoring modules, selection from existing KV entries. Key architectural question: does ReToken's post-encode KV-cache selection match the right computational bottleneck for VLA closed-loop inference, or does surf need pre-encode selection to amortize ViT cost?

## questions

- FA-RDP × [[claims/structure helps]]: FA-RDP's pre-contact phase uses diverse diffusion modes; post-contact phase is reactive. Do surf's line-density patches co-locate with *pre-contact* candidate contact-geometry tokens, while post-contact tokens are primarily identified by motion or force? If yes, the structural prior is phase-restricted — useful for targeting but not for execution-phase token selection.
- FA-RDP × [[claims/iterative selection]] trigger: FA-RDP's contact-onset detector (proprioceptive force threshold or visual motion onset) is a concrete, non-learned trigger. Does this map onto a visually-detectable signal in LIBERO? Or is proprioceptive contact onset more reliable than visual contact onset for triggering the switch from geometric to reactive patch selection?
- ReToken × [[claims/language helps]] scorer design: ReToken's single retrieval embedding selects from a pre-filled KV cache. Surf's current scorer concept selects before ViT encoding. Which is the right bottleneck to target in a 3-7B VLA on LIBERO — LLM attention cost over visual tokens, or ViT encode cost per token? If LLM attention dominates, ReToken's design should be surf's default scorer architecture.
- ReToken × VLA closed-loop: ReToken operates on a frozen KV cache. In VLA closed-loop inference, the visual observation changes at each step. Does the KV cache need to be refilled at every step, or can it be reused across steps for similar observations? If refilled every step, the ViT encode cost is unchanged and ReToken only saves LLM attention — which may or may not be the dominant cost.
- Sridhar et al. × [[claims/structure helps]] generalization: Behavior-aligned reps (EE traces = contact trajectory = spatial-structural signal) improve cross-embodiment transfer. Is the benefit from spatial structure (which tokens the robot contacts) or from action alignment (which motions are semantically equivalent)? A co-location test between EE traces and surf's line-density heatmap on LIBERO frames would distinguish geometric structure from trajectory annotation.
- Dong et al. × surf budget policy training: If surf's budget policy uses online RL fine-tuning, Dong et al. says don't pretrain the Q-function. Does this generalize when the RL action space is combinatorial (subsets of visual tokens) and the task reward is slow (episode-level manipulation success)? The combinatorial action space may make random Q-function initialization impractical without additional shaping.
- WorldBagel × [[claims/iterative selection]]: WorldBagel produces visual predictions alongside actions at each step. Does the mismatch between WorldBagel's predicted next-frame tokens and observed next-frame tokens serve as a more informative iterative selection trigger than CheckVLA's visual-observation mismatch? Or are they equivalent, since both compare predicted vs. observed visual tokens?
