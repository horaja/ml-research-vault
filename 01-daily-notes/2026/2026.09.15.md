**core**

- [GSPA: Geo-Semantic Prior-Guided Attention for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/593fe3f7bbdcbd6169f9903fcab28e8e65316c4e) — Che et al. · SWARM Intelligence 2026
  Uses geometric and semantic priors jointly to guide VLA attention through cluttered scenes, contact dynamics, and stage transitions in long-horizon manipulation. The most direct structural prior baseline in the corpus — SURF must understand exactly what "geo-semantic" means here (depth? edge? learned geometry?) to position the line-drawing prior. Day 93 in backlog; read alongside the positioning note.

- [AffordSim: A Scalable Data Generator and Benchmark for Affordance-Aware Robotic Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1adbe7f56d7a64a14e86c54f8156b0357ee8a4a9) — Li et al. · arXiv 2026
  Scalable simulator where task-success is conditioned on contacting the *functional object region* — the manipulation-relevant contact point rather than a stable grasp. Operationalizes the core evaluation question for [[claims/structure helps]]: does SURF's line-drawing prior identify affordance contact regions better than generic importance/redundancy baselines? Could serve as the eval benchmark for the contrastive alignment experiment — affordance contact region prediction as a proxy for task-relevant patch selection.

- [FocusUI: Efficient UI Grounding via Position-Preserving Visual Token Selection](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/022a6b853a918ddaa7201b40e48d566fc797ed16) — Ouyang et al. · arXiv 2026
  Token selection that explicitly preserves spatial relationships between retained tokens. Directly answers the open question in [[questions]] (architecture section) about whether global-semantic / attention-score selection erodes the positional-interaction frame (@huangNuwaMendingSpatial2026). If FocusUI's position-preserving mechanism generalizes to VLA manipulation, it resolves a key architectural concern about SURF's selector module before it's designed.

**adjacent**

- [On the dynamic evolution of CLIP texture-shape bias and its relationship to human alignment and model robustness](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34d010689667d234226cdec857c77b30a27d62c4) — Hernández-Cámara et al. · 2026
  CLIP develops stronger shape bias and weaker texture bias over training; higher shape bias correlates with human alignment and OOD robustness. Relevant to SURF's implementation context: most VLA architectures use CLIP-based encoders that may retain texture bias depending on training stage. If the underlying encoder is texture-biased, SURF's line-drawing (shape-biased) prior may do more than select useful regions — it may explicitly counteract encoder texture bias as a form of inductive bias correction.

- [Diffusion-based stimulus optimization reveals functional organization across higher visual cortex](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc7def9a20d1dab6e5b64ac46705eede7e28dc9) — Henderson et al. · bioRxiv 2026
  Uses diffusion-based preferred-stimulus synthesis (BrainDiVE) to map topographic functional organization across HVC, extending beyond V1/V2/V4 to anterior visual regions. Extends evidence for [[claims/v4 relevance]]: if action-relevant visual cortex regions are topographically organized (as the action-topography paper from 08-31 suggests), Henderson et al.'s method could directly synthesize the stimulus preferences of action-tuned cortical regions, providing a grounded prior for patch selection in manipulation tasks.

**horizon**

- [ProgResViT: Progressive Resolution and Width for Adaptive Vision Transformers](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b7ced9770043fa24ad21aeff9bcdbee44725fc71) — Hojjat et al. · 2026
  Anytime ViT: first pass at low resolution + narrow subnetwork, exits when confident, refines at higher resolution + full width if uncertain. A hardware-facing instantiation of [[claims/iterative selection]] in pure classification — input-adaptive resolution progression rather than patch selection. The early-exit stopping criterion (confidence threshold on class logits) is a clean baseline for SURF's when-to-refine question: what would SURF's analogous stopping criterion look like in a closed-loop VLA context where task completion is non-binary?
