**core**

- [Training-Free Interaction-Aligned Visual Token Pruning for Efficient Embodied Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a63ff0e77bf727e8235c4fc6768f8f59e5cfcdf4) — Cheng et al. · S2
  Proposes interaction-region-aligned token pruning for robot manipulation without training. Critical question for SURF: what defines "interaction-aligned" here — learned visual features, depth, or structural geometry? If learned, this is a direct baseline SURF must beat on data-efficiency or zero-shot grounds. If the interaction criterion is computable from structural cues, SURF's line-drawing prior could feed directly into this framework.

- [ResPrune: Text-Conditioned Subspace Reconstruction for Visual Token Pruning in Large Vision-Language Models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8d6855727d59b8f55efe9cade6191b073037d6a) — Li et al. · arXiv
  Prunes tokens that do not contribute to a text-defined visual subspace — reconstruction loss under the text-conditioned subspace as the pruning criterion. Mechanistically distinct from SURF's contrastive language-conditioned selection. Directly constrains [[claims/language helps]]: if text-conditioned subspace reconstruction already captures task-relevant structure, SURF's structural line-drawing prior must provide signal orthogonal to language. Read to understand the subspace construction method before designing [[experiments/contrastive alignment]].

- [Focus-Scan-Refine: From Human Visual Perception to Efficient Visual Token Pruning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a1047da25d280a53cc064ab3877b280a707f78b0) — Tong et al. · arXiv
  Decomposes visual attention into three stages — fixation (coarse global), scanning (structured sweep), refinement (fine-grained correction) — grounded in human foveated vision and V1 surround suppression. Biologically motivates [[claims/iterative selection]]: if human token selection is inherently iterative with a stopping criterion in the refinement stage, SURF's multi-round selection claim gains perceptual grounding. Also directly addresses "when to stop refining." Read to extract the stopping criterion design.

**adjacent**

- [Think Proprioceptively: State-Grounded Visual Token Selection for VLA Policies](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ce0a7201ebc0b4804a7f28d3536338e57e5c345) — Wang et al. · S2
  Uses proprioceptive state (end-effector position, gripper state) to gate visual token selection — no instruction text, no structural geometry. Required read for the positioning note: defines what proprioceptive-state-grounded selection achieves and on what axis SURF's structural prior differentiates (zero-shot deployment without proprioceptive loop, or cross-task generalization where the same structural cues serve multiple instructions). This is ThinkProprio; it has been in the priorities list since day 13.

- [Poster: Task-Aware Dynamic Visual Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Inference](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57777db0ec2f567f3742d2d0cab4f1e00613e765) — Lin et al. · ECRTS
  Conference paper (embedded/real-time systems venue) measuring task-aware dynamic token pruning in VLA inference at the system level. The ECRTS venue suggests evaluated latency and accuracy numbers on a real deployment stack. Worth reading to extract empirical numbers on task-aware pruning in VLA: what is the compute budget, how dynamic is the selection, and does task-awareness come from instruction, structural cues, or an independent scorer?

**horizon**

- [Information-dependent eye-hand coordination emerges from active vision](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/439f4faa6ea6f868d768d06c98a2dcdbcc8bfc44) — Zhao et al. · bioRxiv
  Computational/neuroscience paper showing that eye-hand coordination emerges from information-dependent active vision — gaze targets regions of maximal task-relevant information gain, not maximal visual saliency. Directly relevant to [[eval/gaze]] baseline design: if human gaze is information-driven (not saliency-driven), SURF's structural prior should be evaluated against information-theoretic gaze baselines, not just raw gaze maps. Also frames SURF's patch selection as an active information-gathering decision, not a passive filtering step.
