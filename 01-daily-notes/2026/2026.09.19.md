**core**

- [History-Conditioned Spatio-Temporal Visual Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language Navigation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4755e2c70d9686aeb663bcb341c339a8edc6df33) — Wang, et al. · arXiv
  Training-free spatio-temporal token pruning for VLN/VLA that conditions spatial importance on accumulated observation history across steps. Adds a temporal-history axis to the VLA token pruning landscape: SURF (structural geometry), TADP (within-step VL interaction), ThinkProprio (proprioceptive state), and now this (cross-step history). SURF's pre-deployment advantage applies here too: history conditioning requires task rollout history; structural geometry is available before any inference.

- [SkipVLA: Skipping VLA Steps with Classical Planning for Fast Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20648v1) — Agrawal et al. · arXiv
  Classical planning (PDDL-style) decides which VLA inference calls to skip entirely in long-horizon tasks, reducing query count without retraining. New efficiency axis: step-level frequency vs. token-level sparsity within a call. Composable with SURF: classical planning controls *when* VLA is invoked; structural prior controls *what* the VLA sees when invoked. Together these are orthogonal efficiency dimensions.

- [MINERVA: How Small Can a Manipulation Policy Be and Still Solve LIBERO?](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75ee38db94bf920a19799df59cffbda53ca7d54c) — Sendai et al. · S2
  A 0.54M-parameter visuomotor policy matches or exceeds billion-parameter VLA models on LIBERO. Sharpens the positioning question raised on 09-16: if LIBERO is solvable by a compact policy, then token pruning of large VLAs on LIBERO is not measuring what matters. SURF must target tasks where large vision-language pretraining is the bottleneck — generalization to novel instructions, objects, or environments where compact policies fail. LIBERO may not be the right eval benchmark.

**adjacent**

- [Robot Independent Intelligence: When Language Vanishes in Vision-Language-Action Models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b733fcd0d3741c9139ab8e7bccebc5f0fb49933) — Gui et al. · IEEE RA-L
  Systematically removes language from VLA policies and measures the performance floor. Defines the "language dependency" gradient: how much task performance degrades as language conditioning is removed. Direct test of [[claims/language helps]] from the ablation direction — the paper measures what SURF's language conditioning is supposed to beat. If language removal barely degrades performance, the language-conditioned selection axis loses its motivation.

- [Toward Unified Robot Learning: Bridging Representation, Vision-Language-Action, and World Models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/51e63227134934c753dbc3c367cba4ab56ed3002) — Mehta et al. · TMLR
  Systematic survey unifying representation learning, VLA, and world models for robot learning, published in TMLR. Will be widely read. Useful context for the positioning note's related work: how does selective visual computation fit within this three-way taxonomy? The framing question for SURF: is the structural prior part of the representation module, the VLA module, or the world model?

**horizon**

- [Localist Topographic Expert Routing: A Barrel Cortex-Inspired Modular Network for Sensorimotor Processing](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be92a18f2791e12d97f4cff13febdd297ecfcdaa) — Zhu et al. · NeurIPS
  Each sensory domain (whisker/vibrissa) maps to a dedicated cortical column in barrel cortex; this paper reproduces the localist topographic routing computationally. Bridges two literature streams that SURF sits between: topographic V4 organization ([[claims/v4 relevance]]) and language-conditioned sparse expert routing (MoE-ACT, surfaced 09-18). The biological justification for localized topographic routing is the same argument SURF uses for structural patch selection — metabolic / compute efficiency from spatial specialization.

- [PointZero: 3D Point Track Completion for Learning Transferable 3D Dynamics](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65e217d81bdda1bbe80c39e2e362d2880b5bb209) — Duisterhof et al. · S2 (tracked author)
  Learns 3D scene dynamics from human video without robot action labels by completing sparse point tracks; the learned 3D dynamics transfer across embodiments. Adjacent to SURF's pre-deployment framing: if structural geometry can be learned from action-free video, this is a proof of concept that visual structure (3D tracks here, 2D line geometry for SURF) can encode task-relevant dynamics without robot data. The embodiment-transfer result is relevant — structural geometry should be similarly embodiment-agnostic.

## questions

Carried forward (selective):
- S²Prune: does importance ≡ center/texture bias in instruction-conditioned closed-loop VLA, or only static VQA? (unresolved, day 25)
- GSPA: what is the "geo" component — depth, edge, learned geometry? (day 97 unread)
- TADP: pre-deployment / OOD performance of geometry-derived prior vs. VL-interaction-derived importance?
- ThinkProprio: what does structural prior add before any action history exists?
- Gaze on the Prize: structural geometry vs. return-guided contrastive (no prior) OOD?
- Depth Matters: are depth cues and line-drawing cues complementary or redundant for patch selection?

New from today:
- History-Conditioned VT Pruning: does cross-step history conditioning capture the same selection signal as within-step structural geometry, or do they select complementary patches? Can structural prior serve as a history surrogate at step 0?
- SkipVLA: if classical planning determines *when* to call VLA, does the structural prior improve accuracy on exactly those calls (the hard ones that can't be skipped)? Is there a correlation between "non-skippable steps" and "structurally complex scenes"?
- MINERVA: what is the minimal task that requires large VLA pretraining and cannot be solved by a compact visuomotor policy? That task is where SURF's structural prior and efficiency claims should be demonstrated.
