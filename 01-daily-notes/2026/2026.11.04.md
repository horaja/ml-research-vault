## opener

Positioning note is day 71. Unread queue unchanged: Attention from Action, SAPrune, TLC-DiT, SPAR, Cao et al., Shi et al. Stage-Aware. Every downstream spec decision remains blocked.

Three papers today directly bear on SURF's core positioning. ARP (Asynchronous Robot Policies) shows that decoupling a slow semantic VLM from a fast vision-action expert — VLM updating a semantic memory at low frequency, expert generating actions at high frequency — achieves 18% higher success and 10× lower VLM frequency with no performance loss. This is an empirical existence proof that visual semantic processing doesn't need to run at every timestep: a cached structural representation is sufficient for most steps. SURF's structural prior is a form of that cache. Read before writing any VLA efficiency claim.

"Do Agentic Policies Need to Look at Every Step?" (arXiv 2611.05058) is the other key read: proposes 3D geometric abstraction that caches symbolic scene state, refreshes locally, and reduces repeated multimodal queries while improving multi-step manipulation. This is SURF's structural-prior hypothesis from the symbolic side — same empirical conclusion (structural/geometric information is sufficient for many steps), different mechanism (discrete abstraction vs. patch selection). Important to understand the contrast before the positioning note: SURF needs to explain why learned patch selection is better than cached symbolic abstractions.

"Should the Action Expert Train the VLM?" (arXiv 2611.05249) directly maps onto the contrastive alignment spec design question: when gradient insulation from the action expert is removed and action-token supervision is provided, closed-loop performance improves 17% (with action tokens) to 40% (without). The gradient routing decision is SURF's equivalent architectural question for the contrastive prior module.

**core**

- [Asynchronous Robot Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05155) — Shi et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Decouples VLM semantic reasoning from action generation via asynchronous processes: VLM updates a semantic memory at low frequency, lightweight vision-action expert conditioned on that memory runs at high frequency. 18% higher success rate and 10× lower VLM frequency with no performance loss on Franka manipulation. SURF's structural prior is an instantiation of this semantic memory — this paper provides the missing empirical validation that cached semantic context suffices for most action steps, and that the efficiency gain from reducing visual processing frequency is real. Read to extract which visual features the semantic memory caches and whether structural/geometric content is what persists.

- [Should the Action Expert Train the VLM? Action-Token Supervision and Gradient Routing in Mixture-of-Transformer VLAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05249) — Chen et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Disentangles gradient insulation (blocking expert-to-VLM backprop) from action-token supervision (discrete action tokens on the VLM) in VLA training. Without action-token supervision, coupling (allowing expert gradients) reduces trajectory error 25% open-loop and improves closed-loop route completion 40%. With action-token supervision, insulation is competitive but coupling still wins by 17%. Directly maps onto SURF's contrastive alignment spec design: whether to allow the patch-selector's contrastive loss to backpropagate into the VLM backbone, and how action-token supervision interacts with that choice.

- [Do Agentic Policies Need to Look at Every Step? Geometric Abstraction for Reasoning and Action](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05058) — Torres et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Proposes 3D geometric abstraction — typed, evidence-aware symbolic references to object parts grounded from RGB-D — that is refreshed locally after each action. Language model reasons over grounded text rather than raw images at most steps, with direct visual reasoning invoked only on demand. Improves multi-step manipulation completion while substantially reducing multimodal queries. This is the SURF structural-prior hypothesis from the symbolic side: same finding (structural/geometric information suffices for many steps), different mechanism. Positioning note must distinguish SURF's patch-level selection from symbolic abstraction; read to understand when the symbolic approach fails (novel objects, fine-grained manipulation, partial observation).

**adjacent**

- [HiLo-WAM: Decoupling Long-Horizon Task Evolution and Local Dynamics in World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05023) — Zhang et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Sparse long-horizon visual stream (task context, historical evolution) + dense local dynamics stream (short-horizon robot-object interactions) in a dual-DiT architecture with cross-stream attention and progressive inference. 93.70% on RoboTwin 2.0, 2.93× speedup via stream-wise partial denoising. The temporal-scale duality (sparse global / dense local) is adjacent to SURF's spatial-scale question (which patches need full resolution vs. compressed). Read for the progressive inference design: dynamic skipping of one stream when the other is sufficient maps onto SURF's adaptive visual budget.

- [Trusting Imagined Futures: Auditing Imagined Rewards for VLA Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04873) — Kim et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Audits world model rewards for VLA post-training data selection via a multidimensional execution-quality predictor (smoothness, compliance, task progress, stability); high predicted rewards can favor trajectories with poor execution quality without auditing. Adjacent to SURF's [[02-projects/surf/claims/efficiency without collapse]] evaluation: provides a quality-aware reward signal that is execution-grounded rather than reconstruction-based, which is exactly what SURF needs to validate that token selection doesn't degrade action quality in ways that GFLOPs metrics miss.

**horizon**

- [Going Deeper With Memories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05255) — Peng et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  DeepMem: causal multiscale architecture for byte-level LMs using a learned invertible subband transform to construct a hierarchy of shorter sequences; bottleneck refinement costs ~7,600× fewer FLOPs than full-resolution reasoning while maintaining competitive performance. Horizon relevance to SURF: the multiscale compression → iterative bottleneck refinement pipeline is architecturally isomorphic to SURF's structural prior → language conditioning → iterative selection pipeline. The existence proof that computation concentrated at a compressed semantic bottleneck is 7,600× cheaper than full-resolution processing gives theoretical backing to SURF's efficiency claim. The subband transform (frequency-based decomposition) also connects to the spectral token pruning query added Nov 1.
