## opener

Positioning note is day 35. Yesterday's note committed to writing it with explicit gaps — no further deferrals. SAVLA, DualManip, and InternW0-Δ are already in hand as anchors. Today's digest adds a third angle on the "instruction conditioning without structural priors" row: a Knowledge-Based Systems paper that directly implements instruction-conditioned visual token sparsification, the exact design space SURF occupies. This is the fifth or sixth working baseline in that slot. The note can no longer be deferred on grounds of missing context.

The AdaVSkip paper today also clarifies that horizontal token redundancy (fewer tokens) and vertical redundancy (fewer layers) are orthogonal and input-dependent — the implication for [[claims/iterative selection]] is that SURF's structural prior operates only on the horizontal axis, and an unaddressed vertical dimension exists. This is a gap worth naming in the positioning note.

The encoder-free scaling law paper adds a longer-range threat: if visual encoders become unnecessary at sufficient scale, SURF's structural prior module loses its architectural footing. The modular argument needs a version that holds even when the encoder is optional.

- [TaskAnchor: Grounding Task State in Reactive VLAs for Long-Horizon Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b28acc9465c4166824e2cf6930d675e159080f9) — Liu, Zhou, Yue · arXiv · 2026-09-20

- [How Far Are We from Removing the Visual Encoder? Scaling Laws for Encoder-Free Multimodal Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35457v1) — Chen, Ni, Yang · arXiv · 2026-09-28
  Scaling laws comparing encoder-free (raw pixel) vs. encoder-based MLLMs; finds removing the visual encoder shifts compute-optimal balance. Capability threat: if encoder-free MLLMs become competitive at scale, SURF's prior module — which sits inside the encoder — needs a modular justification that survives encoder optionality. The argument SURF needs: structural priors reduce token count at the encoder output, which scales better under constrained compute whether the encoder is pretrained or end-to-end.
