**core**

- [Improving Imitation Learning Efficiency for Manipulation through Geometric Prior Pretraining](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13293120d72f94871506a9c8eb10a7a9613ad60d) — Iwakata et al. · S2
  Uses 3D point cloud geometric prior for pretraining imitation learning policies; reports sample efficiency gains on manipulation without requiring large robot datasets. Directly tests [[claims/structure helps]] from the sample-efficiency angle: if 3D geometric pretraining reduces data requirements across manipulation tasks, the open question for SURF is whether its 2D line-drawing prior captures the same cross-task geometric invariance at inference time rather than pretraining time.

- [GeoLAM: Learning Geometry-Grounded Latent Actions from Unlabeled Human Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17099) — Xie et al. · arXiv 2026
  Separates manipulation-relevant geometric motion from appearance variation in unlabeled human videos to learn latent action representations without action labels. Validates the SURF premise that geometric structure captures action-relevant signal beyond appearance. The differentiation axis vs. SURF: GeoLAM operates on 3D video geometry; SURF's line-drawing prior operates on 2D structural features. Critical question: does the appearance-geometry separation that works in 3D video transfer to 2D patch selection via line drawings?

- [TEMPO: Learning Temporal Context for Dynamic Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16864) — Feng et al. · arXiv 2026
  VLAs fail on dynamic manipulation because single-observation processing misses temporal dynamics; TEMPO introduces temporal context learning across frames. Directly constrains [[claims/iterative selection]]: if temporal context is necessary and sufficient for dynamic tasks, SURF's structural prior (per frame) must offer something temporal history can't — e.g., zero-shot structural grounding before any task experience, OOD generalization when temporal patterns shift. The temporal vs. structural decomposition is a required section in the positioning note.

**adjacent**

- [BrainFocus: EEG-Guided ROI Selection for Efficient Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17443) — Peng et al. · arXiv 2026
  Human EEG signals (neural responses to visual stimuli) guide spatial ROI selection for VLMs; reduces computation while maintaining VQA performance. Adjacent to [[claims/v4 relevance]]: validates the biological-signal → spatial-attention framing from a different modality. Key distinction from SURF: EEG requires a human in the loop; structural priors (line drawings) are data-free. The EEG → ROI mechanism is conceptually analogous to V4 topo map → patch prior — both derive spatial selection from biological neural organization.

- [XPACE: Joint World and Action Modeling from Heterogeneous Experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17372) — Wei et al. · arXiv 2026
  Unified embodied world model jointly predicting robot actions and future video from diverse experience sources. Competing paradigm: if a world model can localize task-relevant regions via predicted future states, structural geometry may be redundant in high-data regimes. The differentiation axis for SURF: structural priors are data-free and available pre-deployment; world models require experience to learn task-relevant localization. This is the strongest paradigmatic competitor to SURF's approach and must appear in the positioning note.

**horizon**

- [AdaVSkip: Adaptive Visual Token Skipping Across Layers for Efficient MLLM Inference](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7888f151de801ebba275622640793848baabfe4) — Sun et al. · arXiv
  Cross-layer (vertical) adaptive token skipping for efficient MLLM inference, complementing horizontal spatial compression. Identifies vertical redundancy as an orthogonal efficiency axis: SURF selects *which* spatial tokens to process; AdaVSkip controls *how many layers* process each token. The two dimensions are potentially composable — a joint spatial × layer efficiency strategy.
