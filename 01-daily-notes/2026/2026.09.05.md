**core**

- [Select, Compress, Reinvest: A Controlled Study of Visual-Token Allocation in Long-Video MLLMs](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/995038e2c94438f095727fdbc18239a009dd447a) — Khatri · arXiv 2026-09-03
  Controlled ablation that isolates *which frames survive* from *how tokens are compressed within a frame* — finds that the frame-selection decision alone accounts for most variance in downstream QA. Direct analog to SURF's spatial vs. temporal budget decomposition: if selection site dominates compression method, SURF's structural prior argument needs to be about WHERE the selector fires, not just HOW MUCH it prunes. Clarifies ablation design for [[experiments/contrastive alignment]].

- [One Demonstration, Many Objects: Generalizing Manipulation via Local Contact Geometry](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bbaf62dc0d38ea8391b602731f7541fc956ecea) — Sharma et al. · arXiv 2026-09-01
  Generalizes dexterous manipulation across unseen objects from a single human demo using local contact geometry as the structural prior. Contact geometry here = fingertip-to-surface regions, not global shape — grounding SURF's structural prior question at the contact scale. If contact-local structure suffices for generalization, then a patch selector guided by contact-region priors should require fewer patches than uniform or saliency-based selection. Updates evidence for [[claims/structure helps]] at the manipulation level, not just the classification level.

**adjacent**

- [TemporalFlow-VLA: Learning Physically Grounded Execution History for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb8b8921d7ce08d5b05263449d79a3f4ad447560) — Yang et al. · 2026-08-27
  Shows that visually similar states at different task stages require different actions — "physical change" tracking via history is needed, not just current frame. Implication for SURF: spatial patch selection that ignores temporal state may select the wrong region at stage transitions. If history-conditioned physical state changes which spatial regions are informative, SURF's prior module needs temporal conditioning, not just instruction conditioning. Informs the architecture question in [[02-projects/surf/questions]].

- [WISE: World-model-guided Imagination Scheduling for Efficient Post-training of VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03681v1) — Zhang et al. · arXiv 2026-09-03
  Uses a world model to schedule *where* to apply imagination during VLA post-training — imagination is concentrated in visually uncertain regions. Complementary to SURF's spatial prior: if a world model identifies uncertain regions for imagination scheduling, and SURF identifies task-relevant regions for attention, these could compose (or compete). The scheduling vocabulary — uncertainty-driven, budget-constrained — mirrors SURF's adaptive compute framing.

**horizon**

- [GazeFS: Target-Centered Gaze-Trajectory Forecasting and Stabilization from Gaze-Head History](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03868v1) — Xia et al. · arXiv 2026-09-03
  Formulates gaze correction as online target-centered trajectory forecasting from head-gaze dynamics history. For [[eval/gaze]]: raw gaze heatmaps conflate target-acquisition dynamics with steady-state fixation — GazeFS provides a decomposition that could sharpen whether SURF's structural prior captures target-acquisition (task-relevant, before fixation) or just the fixation residual (less informative for action selection).

- [TAP-Path: Task-Adaptive Structural and Token Pruning for Efficient and Trustworthy Pathology Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04071v1) — Hasan et al. · arXiv 2026-09-03
  Combines structural pruning (remove tissue-irrelevant encoder blocks) with task-adaptive token pruning in pathology ViTs. Cross-domain evidence that structural + task-adaptive pruning outperforms attention-only or importance-score-only pruning when task-relevant structure is sparse and localizable. Weak analogy to SURF (tissue architecture ≠ manipulation-relevant patches) but the architectural pattern — structural first, then task-gated token selection — is the same.
