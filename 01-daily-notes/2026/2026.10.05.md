# 2026-10-05

## opener

Positioning note is day 41. arXiv is back at 200 papers — first full day after the 2-day 429 outage.

Two new papers today sharpen different corners of SURF's architecture. P2P (Patching to Pruning) proposes depth-wise pruning driven by mechanistic interpretability: only patches that contribute at each transformer layer incur expensive attention and MLP computation. SURF selects which patches enter the model at all (input-stage structural prior); P2P decides at which depth each patch stops getting computed. The interaction is the question: if SURF's structural prior already removes uninformative patches at input, does depth-wise pruning on the remaining selected patches yield additional gains? If not, input-stage selection and depth-wise pruning are substitutes, not complements — which would imply SURF's token budget matters less than where compute is spent per token.

EyeRobot 2.0 implements active visual attention physically: two eyes that swivel to center a 3D fixation point for bimanual manipulation. This is the physical upper bound for SURF's computational approximation. What determines EyeRobot's fixation point is the ground-truth signal SURF's structural prior is trying to predict. If EyeRobot's fixation decisions are learnable from the same structural cues SURF uses, that validates the prior; if they require semantic inference, that sets the ceiling for a purely geometric approach.

"When Text Matters" provides design principles for when language conditioning in token pruning actually helps vs. hurts. Required reading before writing the contrastive alignment spec.

Cao et al. is now day 6 unread. Read today before the positioning note circulates.

**core**

- [From Patching to Pruning Visual Computation in Vision Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03389) — Chowdhury et al. · arXiv
  Depth-wise selective compute: patches that contribute minimally at a given transformer layer are pruned from expensive attention and MLP computation, inspired by mechanistic interpretability. Distinct from SURF's input-stage structural selection — P2P operates per-layer rather than once at input. Key question: if SURF already excludes uninformative patches at input, does P2P on the retained set yield further gains, or are the two methods selecting on the same signal? The answer decides whether structural input selection and depth-wise pruning are complementary or redundant along the visual compute budget.

- [EyeRobot 2.0: Active Gaze for Precise Manipulation without Wrist Cameras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03710) — Hari et al. · arXiv
  Physical active gaze for bimanual manipulation: two camera "eyes" swivel to 3D fixation points, enabling fine-grained contact-level tasks with a single stereo camera and no wrist sensors. This is the physical instantiation of SURF's core hypothesis. The fixation-point selection criterion is the ground truth SURF's structural prior approximates computationally. Read to extract: what signal drives fixation (structural, semantic, or task-conditioned)?

- [When Text Matters: Design Principles for Visual Token Pruning in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34861) — Kang et al. · arXiv
  Empirical analysis of when language-conditioned vs. vision-only token pruning is beneficial; identifies the conditions and failure modes of text-guided selection. Direct grounding for [[claims/language helps]] — what scenes and instruction types are where language conditioning in token selection earns its keep vs. a structural vision-only prior.

**adjacent**

- [XGenAct: Geometry-Enhanced World Action Models through Cross-Task Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03516) — Du et al. · arXiv
  Adds 3D geometric structure via cross-task generation to improve world action models for robot control; geometry improves future prediction in the manipulation domain. Same geometric-structure-helps-manipulation claim as SURF, but at the world model stage rather than input tokenization. Check: 3D geometric world model features (XGenAct) vs. 2D structural selection prior (SURF) — complementary pipeline stages or redundant geometric representations?

- [Decoding the Functional Roles of Register and High-Norm Patch Tokens in Vision Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03698) — Varma et al. · arXiv
  Register tokens suppress high-norm outlier patches that concentrate in background/uniform regions of DINOv2 ViTs; these high-norm patches encode global scene context. SURF's structural prior uses frozen ViT patch features — if the selector discards high-norm background patches as "visually uninformative," it may remove global context tokens the downstream VLA needs. Read to check: do high-norm patches appear predominantly in the low-structural-signal regions SURF's line-drawing prior would deprioritize?

**horizon**

- [Measure Less, Know More: Self-Supervised Test-Time Feature Acquisition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03454) — Jain et al. · arXiv
  Sequential active feature/modality acquisition at test time: selects only informative features under budget constraints using a self-supervised training objective. Formalizes SURF's efficiency/RL module as a sequential feature acquisition problem. The self-supervised acquisition policy is a candidate training recipe for SURF's adaptive budget module that requires no task-specific labels or RL rollouts.

- [Cross-attention encoding models reveal dynamic spatiotemporal routing across human higher visual cortex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36366) — Inabathini et al. · arXiv
  V-JEPA-2 features dynamically routed via cross-attention predict neural responses in dorsal/lateral higher visual cortex better than static routing; routing is spatially and temporally non-uniform. Strengthens [[claims/v4 relevance]]: if learned spatiotemporal routing in a self-supervised video model matches biological routing in dorsal visual areas, then SURF's spatially-structured token selection approximates a biologically-motivated information routing mechanism, not just an efficiency heuristic.

- EyeRobot 2.0: What is the fixation-point selection signal — structural geometry (edges, contact regions), learned task-specific, or semantic instruction following? Does the system use a structural prior, or does it rely on learned visual attention?
