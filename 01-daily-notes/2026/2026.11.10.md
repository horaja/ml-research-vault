**core**

- [Be My Eye: Learning to See Beyond a Robot's Own Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12295) — Huang et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Task-oriented active context sharing for multi-robot VLA: compresses robot observations into compact representations preserving task-relevant information under constrained communication budgets, then integrates into pretrained VLA policy. Supervision: task-oriented future prediction + soft task-progress correspondence. Two-stage training: compression agnostic to collaboration first, then joint with VLA. Demonstrated in dual-robot tabletop tasks. For SURF: de facto published comparison for [[experiments/contrastive alignment]] — task-prediction supervision without structural prior achieves real-robot VLA performance. The direct question before writing the spec: does task-prediction supervision select the same spatial regions as SURF's structural line-drawing prior, or orthogonal ones?

- [Beyond Modality Preference: Controlling Multimodal Arbitration under Modality Conflict in MLLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12883) — Pan et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Identifies a small set of "arbitration heads" in MLLMs that causally control whether predictions follow visual or textual evidence. Training-free inference-time intervention steers these heads toward a designated trusted modality. Stable across calibration sets; bidirectional control across benchmarks. For SURF: [[claims/language helps]] depends on language conditioning biasing which tokens survive. If language conditioning operates through arbitration heads, SURF's contrastive alignment objective should influence these heads — testable diagnostic for where and why language conditioning helps. Also relevant to the [[questions]] entry: global-semantic patch selection may work through these same heads.

**adjacent**

- [SWAM: Scaling World Action Models with Cross-Modal Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12870) — Zhou et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Action supervision degrades language-video alignment in world action models; studies data/supervision design for preserving alignment. Recipe: mixing general video + egocentric video + UMI + robot demos under fixed training budget preserves language-video capability while extending to action. 88.1% (Easy) / 61.6% (Hard) on RoboTwin Clean; 40% on three real-world tasks. For SURF: [[experiments/contrastive alignment]] faces the same alignment-degradation risk. SWAM's data recipe is a concrete mitigation; SURF's contrastive loss must not erode the LLM's visual grounding, or it must recover it via SWAM-style data mixing.

- [dNaViT: Discrete Codes are General-Purpose Visual Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12426) — Li et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Discrete visual tokenizer via grouped product quantization on a frozen pretrained encoder; dual-branch reconstruction + semantic objective; matches or exceeds task-specific baselines across 12 understanding + 4 generation + 1 editing benchmarks. For SURF: a discrete visual vocabulary is a competing efficiency strategy — compression rather than dropping. If dNaViT's discrete tokens naturally cluster task-relevant (geometric vs. texture) information, it becomes a baseline for [[claims/efficiency without collapse]] that collapses spatial resolution without token dropping. Worth checking against SURF's visual budget framing.

**horizon**

- [Depth as a Candidate Source: Portfolio Allocation for Recurrent Test-Time Scaling in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12293) — Hao et al. · arXiv Nov 2026
  Finds that in recurrent latent reasoning, deeper passes can lose correct solutions found at shallower depths; solution sets at different depths are non-nested after controlling for marginal accuracy. Proposes allocating test-time compute as a portfolio across depth levels rather than a single-depth greedy policy. For SURF: this is the clearest published articulation of the case for [[claims/iterative selection]] from first principles — if more computation does not subsume less computation, then coarse-to-fine selection has distinct value beyond compute savings. The portfolio framing also applies to patch selection: a coarse pass selects regions a fine pass may not reach via its own initialization.
