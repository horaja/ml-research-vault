**core**

- [Towards Fine-Grained Object Manipulation: SAM3-Guided Visuomotor Policy with Persistent Memory Learning and Focused Visual Conditioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21621v1) — arXiv 2026-09-18
  SAM3 provides object-level segmentation masks for "Focused Visual Conditioning": the policy attends only to SAM3-segmented task-relevant regions rather than the whole scene. Directly instantiates the segmentation-derived structural prior that SURF's [[claims/structure helps]] must beat. The key comparison: SAM3 uses instance segmentation (semantically grounded, requires object vocabulary); SURF's line-drawing prior is category-agnostic and works from geometry alone. OOD generalization to novel object categories is SURF's differentiation window.

- [Modality-Autoregressive World-Action Models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3395cd60d241d839df377d379639ca85ca7c1d9f) — Hung, Duisterhof, Ramanan et al. · 2026-09-15
  ModAR (Deva Ramanan, tracked author) learns which visual modality — RGB, depth, pretrained features, point tracks — most efficiently captures geometric, semantic, and motion structure for joint future-observation/action prediction. Directly informs SURF's unresolved structural signal choice: depth vs. line drawings vs. features. If ModAR shows point tracks + depth beat RGB for action-relevant geometry, SURF's line-drawing prior needs explicit comparison against both.

- [SABER: Learning Attention-based Semantic Affordance for Legged Locomotion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21572v1) — arXiv 2026-09-18
  Distinguishes terrain geometry (structurally traversable) from terrain affordance (safe for contact in context). Shows that semantic affordance signals beyond raw geometry improve policy safety in industrial environments. Direct evidence bearing on SURF's open question from [[questions]]: "Does line structure help because of affordance relevance, shape invariance, or removal of nuisance detail?" SABER's result suggests that geometry alone is insufficient — affordance semantics (which structure is relevant *for this task*) matters independently. If true for manipulation, SURF's structural prior needs language-grounded affordance annotation, not just line drawings.

**adjacent**

- [Abstention and Noise Filtering: Two Missing Primitives of Softmax Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22005v1) — arXiv 2026-09-18
  Softmax attention structurally lacks two capabilities: abstention (ability to output nothing when tokens are irrelevant) and noise filtering (suppression of off-target activations). Value-gating empirically restores both. Mechanistic grounding for SURF's architectural choice: if softmax cannot abstain from irrelevant visual tokens, an explicit upstream structural prior that pre-selects tokens is architecturally necessary, not just a compute optimization. This may be the theoretical argument SURF needs for why selection before the transformer is better than selection inside it.

- [PRIME: Perception Feedback with Situational Memory Embeddings in VLA Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22040v1) — arXiv 2026-09-18
  VLA for autonomous driving that feeds goal-conditioned situational memory back into early perception, making vision goal-aware rather than processing inputs "agnostically." Closes the perception-reasoning-planning loop. Directly adjacent to [[claims/iterative selection]]: SURF's iterative selection asks whether intermediate task evidence can improve patch selection; PRIME shows goal-conditioned feedback improves early perception in a VLA. The mechanism (feedback from language/goal into the perceptual module) is the architecture-level analog of SURF's language-conditioned prior.

**horizon**

- [World Modeling in Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21748v1) — arXiv 2026-09-18
  Mechanistic analysis of TaxiGPT: behavioral failures that appeared to indicate an incoherent world model are shown (via causal interventions) to stem from search/retrieval failures, not missing representations. The transformer has a faithful world model that it fails to deploy. Horizon relevance to SURF: if the VLA's internal representation faithfully models spatial structure but fails to *retrieve* it for token selection, SURF's structural prior may be solving a retrieval problem rather than a representational one. This reframing changes the evaluation criterion — the question becomes whether the prior improves retrieval of latent spatial representations, not whether it adds new geometric information.
