**core**

- [Learning World Models through Contrastive Identification of Actions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32776) — Lee et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Contrastive training that identifies action-relevant motion outperforms prediction-only objectives for separating action-relevant from task-unrelated visual features; inverse dynamics alone does not achieve the separation. Direct evidence for [[02-projects/surf/experiments/contrastive alignment]]: the contrastive objective is not just an engineering choice but a necessary mechanism for isolating action-relevant structure from background variation.

- [Supply Geometry, Learn Execution: Screw Attention for Zero-Shot Policy Transfer in Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32797) — Magassouba et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Explicit SE(3) geometric prior (screws) + learned execution head achieves zero-shot policy transfer across objects, scenes, and robots without additional training. Structural parallel to SURF: the design philosophy (supply structure, let policy learn action) is the same, but the structural abstraction is 3D kinematic geometry rather than 2D V4 shape. Required for the positioning note competitor table: Screw Attention vs. V4 prior — different spatial representations, different training-distribution requirements.

- [Train What You Run: Probabilistic Convolutions for Learning Token Compaction in State Space Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33009) — Kubaty et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Pre-encoder token removal changes local neighbor structure in SSM backbones (each removal changes which tokens are adjacent for convolutional operations); ProbConv trains the selector to account for this. If SURF's target backbone has convolutional components (Mamba-based VLA, or ViT with local window attention), pre-encoder selection must account for neighbor-structure disruption, not just patch importance. Constraint on the selector training objective.

**adjacent**

- [OmniAct: One Policy, One Million Embodiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33158) — Fan et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Cross-embodiment VLA trained across 1M procedurally designed robot embodiments using per-node kinematic graph conditioning; 63.0% on 2K embodiment-held-out episodes vs. 17.8% for π0.5. Largest-scale structural conditioning result in VLA to date — shows kinematic graph (structural information about the robot) is not just useful but necessary for generalizing across embodiments. Adjacent context for how structural conditioning scales.

- [Knocked Off Course: Tracing Uncommanded Motion from Perception to Action in Embodied VLAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32705) — Zlotnikov et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Traces how visual ambiguity in egocentric VLA observations propagates through the perception-action pipeline to produce uncommanded motion. Empirical evidence for why patch selection matters: feeding the wrong visual tokens to the VLA action head causes incorrect action outputs that are hard to attribute to individual failure modes. Useful for SURF's argument about why pre-encoder selection quality affects downstream action accuracy, not just FLOPs.

**horizon**

- [Benchmarking Evidence Sufficiency in Vision-Language Models for Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32964) — Nguyen et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Formalizes evidence sufficiency — does the available visual context support answering this question? — as a benchmark problem in driving VLMs, separate from localization or prediction accuracy. This is the same conceptual problem as [[02-projects/surf/claims/iterative selection]]'s when-to-stop criterion: the system needs to know when it has seen enough to act. The benchmark design is a reusable evaluation frame for SURF's adaptive budget stopping condition.

- [Scaling Laws of Ensemble Uncertainty in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32801) — Ravanbakhsh et al. · arXiv (Nov 2026)
  Derives scaling laws for epistemic uncertainty estimates in LLM ensembles, showing existing Bayesian DL methods do not scale as expected. Tangential to SURF but relevant to [[claims/iterative selection]]'s uncertainty-driven refinement: if the stopping criterion is uncertainty-based, the scaling properties of the uncertainty estimator matter for how well-calibrated the adaptive budget is at different model scales.
